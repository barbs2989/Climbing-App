# Conditions (route page `forecast` sub-tab, "Your conditions", Best day to climb)

## 1. What it does today
- On a route page the sub-tab id `forecast` is labelled **Conditions**. It shows two different things by discipline:
  - **Crag disciplines (sport, trad, top-rope, bouldering):** a 0-5 star **conditions score**, hour by hour and day by day, with the factor rows, the reason for any cap, the weather tiles (rain amount headline, chance beneath), and a rain timing line ("4 h, 7 PM-11 PM"). The *Day by day* list is drawn as a table: each day is a full-width row with its date beside its name, divided from the next by a `C.border` rule (not the near-invisible `C.borderLight`), and the selected day marked by a green bar on its left edge as well as its tint. A second read of three models' rain (`fetchAlpineSpread`, `_cragSp`) adds "Forecast models disagree on rain this day" under the day's score and "models split" in the day's rain box, by the alpine rule with the printed day total in the comparison; a failed read adds nothing. Rendered by `ConditionsScoreCard` in `RouteDetail.jsx`. Each hourly chart (`HourChartCard`) reads an hour on a **tap**, on a **sideways drag** (at least 8 px across and more than twice as far across as up or down, so it starts at once) or on **touch and hold (350 ms) then slide**; while scrubbing, the cursor line and dots sit exactly under the finger and the boxes above read the nearest whole hour, and on release the cursor glides onto that hour. A swipe that is mostly up or down, even a slightly diagonal one, scrolls the page and never changes the boxes above it (a mouse reads by hover or drag).
  - **Alpine, mountaineering, scrambling, ice, mixed:** **flags and a start time, no stars** (`lib/AlpineConditionsCard.jsx`). DAY BY DAY's values wrap (2026-10-28): at 390px a one-line clip cut every freezing level to "8,200 ft–1…". The start is counted back from the Planner's estimate. On glacier and alpine-ice routes, after a night that froze, it is also never later than one that has the party back off the snow by noon (`LIMITS.snowBackBy`, the guides' practice: Easton leaves high camp 2-5 AM); the card then names the later start the forecast alone would allow, and shows the route's own `timing.recommendedStart` when it has one. The day strip says which day every section below shows, and every day section's heading (the flags card itself, alerts, hour by hour, snowfall, air, sun and shade) has its own ‹ › that steps the same day for all of them (`DayHead`). Under the flags card, **DAY BY DAY** (`DayByDay`, owner's note 2026-10-24) lists all seven days the way the crag card's Day by day does, drawn as the same table (full-width rows with the date, `C.border` rules, the selected day tinted with a blue left edge): each row the day's flag count, high / low, freezing level, **rain** (the day's total from `daySummary`/`wetSpan`, with its wet hours, their window "1 PM–4 PM" and the peak chance beneath; labelled *Precipitation* with "snow melted in" on a snow day — owner's note 2026-10-28: a chance alone cannot tell a shower from a soaking) and gusts at the forecast's height, in a 2 x 2 grid, new snow and the start (the start box's own: none on a minimum estimate or past a one-day push); tapping a row opens that day in every section. The day strip's tiles are the crag strip's size (58 px). It is not sticky: `#appscroll` grows to its content and the document scrolls, so a sticky child cannot stick inside it. With no summit pin, high point or area elevation (4,903 of 6,208 alpine-kind routes, census 2026-10-29), the card uses the ground height Open-Meteo reports for the map point (`groundFt`) for the summit flags and the "Read at … , the ground height at the map point" line. Also fog/visibility, snowfall and snow level, nearest USGS stream gauge, and report freshness.
- Under the score (crag) or the flags card (alpine), above the weather forecasts, the Conditions tab carries **ASPECT & SUN** (which way the face points — the route's own `aspect`, else the crag's `areas.aspect`, the same two the score reads, `src/components/AspectSunPanel.tsx` — in sun or shade now, climber sun reports) and **SEASONAL GUIDANCE** (the best window and month by month), moved there from Overview / Plan on 2026-10-23. Placement is decided in `RouteDetail.jsx` (`condPanels`, `condTab`). After the weather forecasts comes **CLIMATE & SEASON** (`ClimateSeasonCard`, `climateEl`: the route's `climate` column — typical weather, spring/summer/fall/winter, forecast zone — or the *No season guidance* note), moved there from Plan / a crag's Overview on 2026-10-24; a discipline with no Conditions tab (aid) keeps it on Overview.
- **Forecast at key points / Forecast at the crag** (`WeatherPanel` in `src/components/WeatherPanel.tsx`): one card per point, one tile per day (high/low, feels like, AM/PM/Night, wind, precip, UV, freezing level, snow). The NWS and MET rows print **only on a day that service's temperature differs by 6° or more** (`diverges` / `metDiverges`), amber, temperatures only (their winds are 10 m figures against the 80 m headline, so a wind side by side read as a disagreement that is not one); a day the services agree prints neither (owner note "too much text below it", 2026-10-28). The hourly list has no "At/During" explainer line — each box already says "At" or "During" with its own time — and the link list below has no caption.
- **Weather & mountain forecasts** (the outside-forecast link list under the live forecast, `wxEl` → `forecastLinksOf()` in `RouteDetail.jsx`) opens every site that takes a coordinate at ONE point: the point the in-app forecast above it reads (`cragWxPointOf()` — the crag's coordinate — on a crag route, else `forecastPoint()`, the summit pin or the area at the high point). NWS point forecast + hourly graph (`MapClick.php?lat=&lon=`), Mountain-Forecast.com through the site's nearest-peak redirect (`/location_search?lat=&lon=`), and AirNow's fire-and-smoke map centred there (`fire.airnow.gov/#9/lat/lng`). The country comes from the area's `path` (`countryOf()`; seed areas walk `MOUNTAINS`): a Canadian climb gets Environment Canada's point forecast (`weather.gc.ca/en/location/index.html?coords=`) instead of NWS, and InciWeb (US incidents only) is offered only in the US, opening its incident table filtered to the climb's own state (`usStateNameOf()`; `/accessible-view?field_state_target_id=<State>` — its `/state/<name>` pages render empty); an unknown country keeps the US links. OpenSnow was removed (2026-10-24): it has no coordinate URL, so it could only open a search box. With no coordinate at all the sites open on their home pages.
- **Best time to climb** (crag): the big number is the **overall score at the hour you pick**, captioned "Overall score at 3 PM"; the strip under it is headed **Pick an hour** ("daylight hours · colour is the overall score") — one tile per daylight hour, each the same blended score, never a separate per-hour metric (owner's note, 2026-10-24: "Score by hour" read as a different score). The tiles sit on **one clock axis shared by all seven days** (`hrLo`/`hrCol`/`hrGrid` in `ConditionsScoreCard`), so every tile is the same width and an hour is in the same column every day — sized by the day's own count, an evening's last hour stretched across the card. The best 3 hours are a green underline row, not a shadow on the tiles (it made them look taller). Every tile is its own hour's score, caps included, and a tapped tile's label, colour and *Why* are that hour's, never the day's: a still-wet hour is held down by `WET_BANDS` and reads red/amber. When some daylight hour of the day is wet and the rock dries later that day, a line under the day's score says **"Rock likely wet until 11 AM: the hours before it score lower than the day"** (`wetTill`, owner's note 2026-10-28: red morning tiles beside a Good day read as a contradiction), and each wet tile's spoken label adds "rock about 40% dry". A dew-capped morning gets the same kind of line, "Air at its dew point until 10 AM" (`dewTill`): in an audit of 18 crags (2026-10-28) most Good days with red mornings were dew, not rain. The alpine showers flag gives the amount and window beside the chance ("60% chance of rain, about 0.12\" over 3 h (1 PM–4 PM)"). Surfline's hourly rating bars are the model: equal bars on a fixed time axis, colour = the overall rating.
- **Season** (crag, `SeasonChart` in `src/components/SeasonChart.tsx`, rated by `monthFit` in `lib/conditionsScore.js`): five years of monthly average highs and lows at the crag, each month *Prime*, *Good* or *Off season* for the climber's temperature range. A month is judged by its **climbing day**, from the morning (a fifth of the way from the average low to the high, `monthDay`) up to the afternoon high, not by the high alone: a hot month is climbed in the morning and evening. *Prime* = that day within 3° of the range, an afternoon at most 24° above it, and a dry month (under 12 wet days, or under 16 adding up to under 4 in.); *Good* = within 6° and under 16 wet days. Until 2026-10-24 the afternoon high alone decided it, which called July and August off across Canada (owner's note: "too picky … based on temperature alone"); measured on the archive after the change, sport/trad are prime June–September at Squamish, Lake Louise and Bon Echo, while Red Rock, Bishop and Joshua Tree stay off in midsummer.
- `conditions` (labelled **Reports** / "Send Reports") is a different sub-tab: what climbers reported, summarised by `buildConsensus(route.activity)`. It is not forecast-driven and does not feed the score.
- **Your conditions** (`lib/CondPrefs.jsx`): temperature range (all climbing, plus optional per-discipline for sport/trad/toprope/bouldering), wind range, humidity ceiling, sun/shade preference, smoke sensitivity, daylight window (one two-handle range, 3-hour marks under the track), and rain caution ("normal"/"cautious"). **Alpine** and **Ice & mixed** are two further scopes with no presets and no default; if set they add one plain line on the alpine card and never change a flag or start time.
- **Best day to climb** (`lib/BestDayTile.jsx`): the same score run over the climber's saved (`wishlist`) crag routes **and saved areas** (`bookmarks`). A saved crag is scored when one of its `disciplines` is a crag one (for `dominant_discipline` if that is one); a saved region, whose own row has no disciplines, when crag climbs are at least half of the climbs under it (`area_climb_stats`, read by `useAreasClimbDiscs` in `lib/db.js`), for its most-climbed crag discipline. An area is scored at its own coordinate with its own `aspect` and `rock`; peaks, ranges and states are left out. Area rows share the 8-crag forecast limit and open the area page, not a day. It renders inline on Home under Jump back in, and the Menu (Plan & log) opens it as a full-screen overlay (`bestDayOpen` in `ClimbMatch.jsx`) with a Back button; saved climbs with no crag location are listed as unscored; with no saved crag climb it shows only how to add one.

## 2. How it works
1. **Fetch** (`lib/forecast.js`): `fetchCragForecast(lat,lng)` (Open-Meteo hourly; every Open-Meteo forecast fetch asks for `BLEND` — the NWS National Blend of Models first, the default model filling what it lacks — folded back into plain series by `blendModels`, so a forecast does not swing between visits) plus `fetchCragAir` (air quality) and `fetchCragClimate` (archive, monthly climate). Results are cached per crag in module maps in `RouteDetail.jsx` (`_cragAir`, `_cragClim`) and in `BestDayTile.jsx` (`_wx`, max `MAX_CRAGS` = 8 crags; routes on one crag share a forecast).
2. **Score** (`scoreForecast(forecast, wall, prefs, nowMs, air)` in `lib/conditionsScore.js`): five factors with `WEIGHTS` temp 30, humidity 20, dry rock 25, wind 10, sun 15. Each is rounded to whole points before summing; a factor with a missing input is left out and stars = 5 x points / measured points. No clamp.
3. **Dry rock**: a drying credit accumulates since the last rain at `DRY_RATE` (damp 0.6, night 1, rh90 0.6, rh75 0.9, rhDry 1.15, wind 1.15, sun 1.3), scaled by `rainScale(rain24)` and compared to `DRY_HOURS` for the rock family (`lib/rockType.js`: granite/quartzite 12 h, basalt/limestone/metamorphic 24, volcanic 36, sandstone/shale 48, unknown 36; x1.5 if rain caution is "cautious"). Rock type comes from the route's own `routes.rock`, then `areas.rock` (`routeRock`).
4. **Caps** (stars = min(factor stars, lowest cap)): rain in the past hour -> 1; wet sandstone -> 1; `WET_BANDS` = under 30% dry -> 1, under 70% -> 2, not fully dry -> 3.5; air at its dew point -> 2 (`DEW`, 2026-10-29: within 2°F or RH 95%+ always; within `DAMP_SPREAD` 5°F only when calm, under 6 mph, on a clear night, or the morning after one until the wall has had 2 h of sun — the flat 5° rule capped sunny desert mornings; on 17 catalog crags Good days with red mornings fell 12 -> 10, the rest west walls in morning shade); AQI over the limit -> 1, over the lower limit -> 2 (`AQ_LIMITS`, per climber sensitivity). Label via `starsLabel`: 4+ Good, 2.5+ Fair, else Poor.
5. **Wall in sun**: `aspectBearing`, `sunPosition`, `wallInSun`, `sunRiseSet` use the date, the wall aspect and cloud.
6. **Alpine** (`lib/alpineConditions.js`): `condKind` picks the kind per route; `dayFlags` builds flags from `LIMITS`; helpers `fogHours`, `snowHistory`, `snowLevel`, `softensAt`, `freezeHours`, `thunderOnset`, `modelSpread` (3 models: gusts, highs, freezing level, and since 2026-10-28 the day's rain total — flagged when the models are 0.5" or more apart and the wettest is at least twice the driest, `LIMITS.spreadRain`; measured p90 at 12 summits, and the days it catches are the ones where NWS's own QPF sat far below ours, e.g. Baker 0.04"–1.56"; or when one has a wet day, 0.2"+, and another a dry one, under 0.02", `spreadRainWet`/`spreadRainDry`. The amount the card prints, the NBM blend's day total, joins the comparison, `modelSpread(sp, date, null, shownRain)`: in the 2026-10-29 audit on catalog routes the blend alone was wet, e.g. Mount Adams 0.29" against 0.00–0.09" from the three models and 0.01" from NWS), `alertsForDay` (NWS alerts), `sunClimbStart`, `wholeDayLegs`. Local time is zone-aware via `localFn` (so DST days are 23-25 h). `lib/streams.js` finds the nearest gauge within 25 km and reports the last 24 h; it states no safe/unsafe verdict.
7. **Prefs store**: `updateCondPrefs` -> `cleanPrefs` -> device localStorage + subscribers + debounced save to the account (`useCondPrefsAccount(uid)`; `saveMyCondPrefs`/`useMyCondPrefs` in `lib/db.js`). Account copy wins on sign-in; an account with none adopts the device's; a device last owned by another account starts from defaults. Temps are stored in F and wind in mph regardless of display units. Sync states: local/loading/saving/saved/failed.

## 4. Data
- **External, called from the browser:** Open-Meteo forecast, air-quality and archive APIs; `api.weather.gov` (points, alerts); `waterservices.usgs.gov` (site list and `iv` flow, parameters 00060/00065).
- **Supabase:** `areas.rock`, `areas.rock_basis` (migration 0248; 'stated' or 'mapped', read by the Rock tile and the score; both render the same, with no "(mapped)" label since 2026-10-15); `areas` aspect (0249); `profile_condition_prefs` (migration 0290: one owner-only row per climber, `prefs jsonb` with keys rain, temp{all,sport,trad,toprope,bouldering,alpine,ice}, wind, rhMax, sun, aq, window).
- **Not stored:** forecasts, scores and flags are computed per view and cached only in memory; nothing is written to the DB. The `conditions` tab's consensus is derived, not stored.

## 5. Connections
- Route page: sub-tab `forecast`; BestDayTile's `onOpen(r, sub)` sets a pending sub-tab and calls `openRoute`.
- Planner: alpine start time uses `planTimes` (`lib/planTimes.js`).
- Home *Finish setting up*: a *Set your conditions* row while `condPrefsSet()` is false (nothing chosen) and `useCondSync()` is past "loading"; it opens `condsOpen`.
- Settings/Menu: `CondPrefsControls` (`only="temp"` on the alpine card); BestDayTile `onPrefs` opens it.
- Units: displays through `uTemp/uWind/uElev/uSnowfall/uTempDelta`; `check:units` covers it.
- Reports: separate tab; alpine card shows report freshness from trip reports.
- Crag routes have no Plan tab (conditions live on Overview/Conditions, per project notes).

## 8. Known gaps / open decisions
- No forecast is saved for offline use (owner, 2026-10-21): offline, every forecast surface says it could not load. Past ~day 11, and for freezing level/UV/low cloud past day 4, the series is the default model (GFS in the US), which swings more.
- `DRY_HOURS` is published guidance and was left unchanged in #2406; the best fit wanted night to dry faster than day and was not adopted (PR text).
- No alpine go/no-go standard exists, so alpine is flags only by owner decision (2026-10-07).
- No published fog/whiteout standard for model visibility in mountains; the card never says "whiteout" (PR #2352).
- Gauges may be on another stream; no wadeability verdict is given (`lib/streams.js`).
- #2406 notes a full local build was killed for low memory; CI was the full run.

The forecast carries no "read it yourself" or "sources can disagree" caveats (owner, 2026-10-15 #2425 and 2026-10-24): the only footer kept is the alpine hour-by-hour's measured station temperature error. Neither hour-by-hour section carries a how-to-read caption either (owner, 2026-10-24): the crag one's "At 2 PM / During 1–2 PM / Drag along a chart" box and the alpine one's "Key times for this day, then each hour at … Tap an hour, or step with ‹ ›" line are gone — each tile already says "at 2 PM" or "1–2 PM", and the alpine card states its height in its "Read at …" line.
