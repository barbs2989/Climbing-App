# The route page

Part of the current-state docs. The Conditions tab has its own doc: [conditions.md](conditions.md).

## 1. What it does today

- Tapping a climb anywhere in the app opens its page (`tab="routes"` with `selRoute` set). The header carries the five rating stars with the vote count beside them ("N votes", or "No votes"; Mountain Project's "from N votes", AllTrails' "(N)"), a ★ cell with the average over "★ N votes" (the same count), grade, discipline and save/log actions. The discipline badges come from `rDiscs` (via `DiscBadges`): the route's primary (`catOf`) first, then the other disciplines its `disciplines` list names, never "Scrambling" on a route with a 5.x grade.
- **Jump links** (`SectionJumps` in `ClimbMatchCore.jsx`): a pill row at the top of every sub-tab, one pill per section heading (4+ only). A tap jumps instantly and flashes the heading; the heading lands 12px **below** the sticky app bar, measured at tap time, never under it (2026-10-21: a fixed 12px margin hid it behind the 59px bar).
- **Section headings carry the map legend's icon** where the whole section is one waypoint type: CAMPING & BIVY the purple ⌂ (Campsite), APPROACH the ⇢ (Approach), GETTING THERE the green ◈ (Trailhead). `SL`'s `wp` prop draws `wpGlyph`/`wpColor` from `WP_STYLE`, the same table the route map's legend and pins use, so they cannot drift. Mixed sections (WAYPOINTS, DESCENT) take none.
- **The twelve waypoint colours are spread for distance, not just hue** (`WP_STYLE`): the seven familiar ones (green trailhead, yellow junction, blue water, purple campsite, orange summit, red hazard) plus a pale sky Topout, aqua Base, pale stone Crag, dark olive Pass, chartreuse Approach and pink Landmark. The closest pair is 19.3 in CIEDE2000; `check:wp-styles` fails any pair under 18. A recorded pin's glyph takes white or near-black, whichever contrasts more with its fill (`wpInk`).
- Sub-tabs: `overview`, `planner`, `forecast` (labelled "Conditions"), `conditions` ("Reports" / "Send Reports"), `safety`, `partners`, `photos`. **Crag routes have no Plan tab** (see `CRAG_CATS`, `showPlan`); the Safety tab is unconditional. The sub-tab bar scrolls sideways, and on a phone its last tabs (Partners, Photos) sit past the right edge; while a tab is hidden off either end, that edge fades out and carries a round **‹ / ›** button that scrolls the bar (`tabEdge`, `nudgeTabs`, 2026-10-23 — the MUI scrollable-tabs / Notion pattern). A bar that fits shows neither.
- Your header stars persist. Tapping another star changes your rating; tapping your own star again withdraws it. Signed out (DB builds), a tap lights nothing: it says "Sign in to rate this climb" and opens sign-in. It used to light the stars while saving nothing, so the ★ cell and the count below never moved and the page contradicted itself. Signed in, the write refreshes `route-star-summary`, so the ★ cell and both counts move as soon as it saves. There is no minimum-votes filter on the Climbs list: Mountain Project's route finder filters by minimum stars only, and the list keeps to its set.
- Every figure built from several climbers' input states how many climbers it comes from and over what window (e.g. "avg of N ratings", "N climbers logged it", "N in the last 45 days", "N of 3 suggested").
- **ROUTE STATS** (`RouteStats`, `lib/RouteStats.jsx`, 0353; DB path only): a card at the foot of Overview, just above *Quick log* / *Full log*, modelled on Mountain Project's sidebar and Stats page and theCrag's season chart. Four tiles: **Page views** (total, and how many in the last 30 days), **Sends** (*Sends / summits* off crags; by N climbers), **Logged climbs** (by N climbers, date of the last one) and **On objectives** (climbers who saved it); then the **star breakdown** (5★ to 1★, the same votes as the header's "N votes") and **sends by month** (Jan–Dec bars over dated sends). A view is one visitor, one route, one UTC day: `recordRouteView` runs once when the page opens, on any sub-tab; the server takes a signed-in viewer from `auth.uid()` and a signed-out one from a random key kept on the device, and stores only an md5 of it. Private logs count as numbers with no name (Mountain Project's private-tick model, as in 0329). A send is what Ranks calls one (not Attempt, Turned around, Fell, Hung, Project or Conditions); a Conditions report is not a logged climb. A failed read says *Route stats aren't reachable right now*, never zeros. The chip strip under the header still counts only the reports you can read, because it opens them.
- **BETA leads to the climb, and every ROUTE BREAKDOWN box opens** (owner note 2026-10-22): the Overview BETA card ends in *Follow the climb section by section*, naming the section and its count (*Route breakdown · 5 sections · on the Plan tab*); it switches to Plan (crag routes stay on Overview) and scrolls to CLIMBING ROUTE (`#climbing-route`) when one is written, else to ROUTE BREAKDOWN (`#route-breakdown`). Shown only when that section has rows. In `RouteBreakdown` every row opens: a travel leg (gully, rappel, snowfield) gets *✎ Edit this section*, only the Terrain / Length / Anchor tiles that hold a value, and *Comments on this section* (`PitchComments`, `title` prop). *Edit this pitch/section* passes the row's `pitchDetail` index and the editor finds the row by `data-src`. It used to pass the pitch-only count, which on a mixed route opened the wrong row. Mountain Project's topos tie each pitch's beta to that pitch the same way.
- Planner (alpine layout) has a **Full Trip Time** card: Approach, Climbing, Descent, Total trip (approach + climb + descent).
- **ACCESS & REGULATIONS is a dropdown, closed by default** (`AccessFold`): one row shows the heading and the labels of what it holds (e.g. "Permit · Fees · Land manager"); tapping it opens the permit, fees, closures, rules, land manager, pass, parking and permit links. The pencil stays beside it. The body is hidden, not unmounted, so the text stays in the markup. It sits `FOLD_GAP` (24px, twice `ROUTE_GAP`) below the card above it, so the closed row does not read as part of the trailhead cards; the Safety tab's two dropdowns (`FireNearRoute`, `FloatPlanFold`) take the same gap above and below.
- Plan tab, GETTING THERE: under the `TrailheadCard` (name, drive directions, *Drive here*, copyable coordinates) sits `SummitLegsCard` — **Trailhead to summit** (time, gain, distance) and **Summit to trailhead** (time, distance), the trip-profile layout of The Mountaineers' route pages. Times are `planTimes()` at its defaults, so they match Full Trip Time; gain only where it is the whole climb (alpine/mountaineering/hiking, or gain ≈ loss); per-leg distance only on an out-and-back (a loop or point-to-point gives the whole outing once); a whole-day-only stored time renders one **Car to car** row. Never on a crag route. `check:trailhead-directions` section 9.
- Plan tab, THE CLIMB, in a climber's order: APPROACH → **CLIMBING ROUTE** (`ClimbingRouteTable`, the climb at a glance, section by section) → **ROUTE BREAKDOWN** (`RouteBreakdown`, every pitch and leg) → DESCENT → rappels. Overview before detail, as guidebooks print a route (owner, 2026-10-22). CLIMBING ROUTE renders wherever `climbing_route` is written, pitched routes included (it was hidden on the 46 alpine routes that carry one, and drawn AFTER the breakdown on 121 scramble/mountaineering routes); its empty *Describe the climbing* state stays unpitched-only. When both are on the page, `BreakdownHandoff` joins them: a short spine under the overview and "↓ Pitch by pitch below — N roped pitches and M legs in the route breakdown.", counted by `breakdownRows()`. No section is matched to a pitch: nothing stored says which pitches a section spans.
- RAPPELS (`RappelTable`): **each rappel is a dropdown, like a camp under CAMPING & BIVY** (owner note 2026-10-24, to save screen space). `RappelRow` always shows R#, length and anchor, plus a red *N hazards* count when the station has any; *▸ More* opens finding the station, hazards, the pull and notes. The first rappel opens expanded, as the first camp does. A row with none of those has no toggle.
- Climbers can edit/correct route fields through edit pencils; every edit is held until the owner approves it (see [route-quality-control.md](route-quality-control.md)).
- Crag routes show `CragLocationCard` (crag + parking coordinates, Google/Apple Maps). Its *Add parking*, *Add the pin* and the pencil on a parking pin open the `waypoints` pin editor (map picker + lat/lng). That editor is offered on every crag discipline. On a crag it is labelled *Parking spot* and its only type is the parking pin (`WP_TYPES_CRAG`; a crag climb carries no waypoints), and the stored `Trailhead` type is shown as *Parking* (`wpTypeLbl`). `trailheadPoint()` reads that pin before the crag-wide `areas.parking_*` pin.
- **A class range is one grade plus a notice.** A stored range such as "Class 3-4" or "3rd-4th class" headlines its top (`displayGrade` / `cruxGrade` in `lib/grade.js`), which is the grade `grade_num` and the finder filter already use; each Composite Grade chip shows one grade (`singleGrade`: the route's headline class when the range contains it, else the top), and `gradeRangeNote` writes the spread under the chips: *Graded Class 4 for its hardest moves; easier sections down to Class 3*, or, when the headline is below the range top, *Graded Class 3 on its standard line; some sections or variations reach Class 4*. Only the head of a value counts; a range inside its qualifier stays prose.
- On **sport, trad and top-rope** routes the grade is shown once, in the Composite Grade panel of ROUTE FACTS / TECHNICAL STATS (`TechStats`). The *Crux grade* tile appears beside it only when a stored crux grade differs from the grade that panel shows; every other discipline keeps the tile as before.
- **The header strip's season is a short window in one spelling**: `seasonShort()` (RouteDetail.jsx) first runs `seasonTidy()` — a month pair is cut to three letters joined by a plain hyphen, a qualifier is its own word, the first letter is a capital (`"Mid-July to September"` → `"Mid Jul-Sep"`) — and skips a window its own clause negates (`"avoid May-Sep"` is never shown as the season). The Approaches pill uses the same function at 48 characters. The prose under the season chip and under an approach pill compares against `seasonTidy()` of the full text, so a respelled window does not print twice.
- **Every approach distance says which distance it is.** `dist_km` is the walk in, ONE WAY (a recorded loop or point-to-point holds the whole outing, `effDistIsWholeTrip`). The header strip prints the same figure and label as TechStats' tile (`headlineDist`: *Round trip* = doubled, *Total distance*, or plain *Distance* for an unrecorded shape the name guesses is not out-and-back); add-a-climb (`lib/AddRoute.jsx`) labels its box *Approach, one way* or, once *How you get back* is Loop / Point to point, *Total distance*, under the shared `HINTS.dist` (lib/fieldHints.js) that says one way vs whole outing; SuggestFix's box reads the same; a crag type (no distance box: SuggestFix `DROP` keeps `dist` off crags) has its Approach box ask for the distance and minutes by example; Compare's Approach row ends *one way* / *whole outing*; each way in's distance box says *one way*. `uDist` prints one decimal in both units and two under 1 (metric used to print the raw number — "7.564 km").
- The guidebook's 0–4 stars (`guide_stars`) show in ROUTE FACTS / TECHNICAL STATS as **Star rating** — how good the climb is. It was labelled just "Quality", which read as rock or protection quality beside *Rock quality* and *Protection*. The rock report tags' group is likewise **Rock quality** (Solid, Loose, Polished…), matching ice's *Ice quality*.
- **The route map's dashed line joins only the pins on the way** (`waypointLinePins`, `lib/track.js`). It is drawn when a route has no recorded track, or its stored `gpx` is only its own pins joined up (`trackIsJustTheWaypoints`). It runs from ONE trailhead (the one at mile 0, else the first listed) through the pins whose `distMi` puts them past it, in distance order, to the Summit/Topout/Crag. A junction, pass or base with no distance goes where it adds the least length. Left off the line, though still drawn as pins: another trailhead (another way in), a camp or water with no distance (it may be a campground by the road), anything before the trailhead, and a camp or side stop whose detour is over 1 km and over 60% longer than its leg. As in Gaia GPS and CalTopo, a waypoint marks a place and does not have to sit on the line (owner, 2026-10-23). Before this, every pin was joined in `distMi ?? 0` order, so a summit with no distance sorted to mile 0 and the line ran trailhead → summit → back down to the camps.
- **Tapping a pin on the route map opens ONE card** (`GPXMap`): the tapped waypoint only. Pins drawn within a fingertip of it (22px at the current zoom) are an *Also here* row of buttons under it; a button swaps the same card to that pin and moves its tip there. The card is capped to fit inside the map. It used to stack every nearby pin's full card in one popup, which read as two overlapping boxes and ran off the bottom of the 300px map.
- **Base of the climb** (`BaseCheckinPanel`, 0188, 0363) sits directly under the route map (ROUTE TRACK on the alpine Plan, CRAG MAP on crag Overview). A climber standing at the start taps *I’m at the base — check in*; the phone takes a steady GPS fix and `check_in_at_route_base()` decides whether it is at this climb (within 500 m of a crag, 5 km of a peak, or 600 m of a Base pin — any of the 11 Base spellings, `route_base_wp_type()`, the same set as `WP_TYPE_MAP`). Accepted check-ins are stored per ROUTE, one per climber. **The consensus is the database's** (`route_base_spots`, 0363): the check-in with the most others within 25 m seeds a spot, its members are those within 25 m of it, its centre is their MEDIAN, and the next spot is found from what is left (order-independent). The top spot is **confirmed** when at least 3 climbers stood in it AND they are a strict majority of the route's check-ins. **A confirmed spot feeds back into the route's own Base pin** (`apply_route_base_consensus`, run by a trigger on every check-in, re-check-in or withdrawal): it fills a Base pin with no coordinate, appends one when the route has none, or moves the pin when it is within 200 m (dropping a stored elevation that described a spot more than 25 m away). A confirmed spot more than 200 m from the pin is **held** for the owner, not applied. Every placement, move and hold is a row in `route_base_pin_changes` with the waypoint as it was. Withdrawals never move a pin back. The spots are drawn ON the map as ringed Base pins (`GPXMap`'s `baseSpots`, labelled confirmed or not), and *How far am I from the base?* gives a live distance and compass direction to a confirmed spot, else the route's Base pin, else an unconfirmed spot (said so), else (crags only) the crag's coordinate (`baseGuide` in `lib/baseCheckin.js`). Inside GPS error it says you are there and shows the route's `location` note, which is what tells neighbouring lines apart. There is no other way to set the start: the map-tap *Start of climb* form (`start_location` contributions) was removed on 2026-10-23 as an unverified, competing consensus.
- **NEXT DOOR** (`WallNeighbours`, crag routes except bouldering, DB path): right under BASE OF THE CLIMB (after WHERE IT STARTS), never on a boulder problem (owner, 2026-10-21: problems are not climbed along a wall in an order), the climbs either side of this one on its wall by `routes.sort_order` (left-to-right position), each a tap to open, with "#n of N from the left on this wall"; *Leftmost / Rightmost climb* at the ends. It reads the crag's thin rows the page already loads for *More on <crag>*, so it costs no query, and draws nothing for a climb with no position. Positions come from Mountain Project's area pages (`scripts/pipeline/order-from-mp.mjs`, see `docs/features/maps-and-area-browser.md`).
- **ON THIS BOULDER** (`BoulderNeighbours`, bouldering only, DB path): in NEXT DOOR's place on a boulder problem, *← PREVIOUS* / *NEXT →* step to the other problems and variations on the same boulder (the problem's own area, as Mountain Project files it), with "Problem n of N on <boulder>" and *First / Last problem* at the ends. No left/right or "next door" wording. Order is the stored `sort_order` (MP's) first, then problems with none by name. When the area's name says it is a group of boulders (`BOULDER_GROUP_RX`: sector, area, field, circuit…) it reads **IN THIS AREA** / "in <area>" instead, so a sector is never called a boulder. Same thin rows (`cragSibs`), no query; non-bouldering rows in the area are left out.
- On a crag route the map section is headed **CRAG MAP** (alpine family: ROUTE TRACK): it is a location map — where the crag is and where to park — not a track of the climb, and its one-line intro says so. When a GPX line exists it is the walk from parking to the base of the crag, on to this climb's start when it sits apart from the main wall; the climb itself is not part of it. The empty state is *No trail to the crag yet* → *Add the trail*, which opens `GpsSubmissionModal` with `crag` set (heading *Add the trail to the crag*, and where to start and stop recording). On a crag with a parking pin or a crag coordinate that sheet opens on **Draw on map** (`TrailDrawMap`, `lib/TrailDrawMap.jsx`): the climber taps the trail from **P** (parking) to **C** (the crag) over Topo or Satellite, with Start at P / Finish at C / Undo / Clear and the length in their units; it is sent as `device_type` *Traced on the map* through the same reviewed path as a recording (`validate-gps`, then a person approves, then `routes.gpx`). The crag sheet's checkboxes ask about walking the trail, not climbing the route.
- Safety tab ORDER (owner, 2026-10-24): the forecast (`wxEl` — the live forecast and *Weather & mountain forecasts*, on EVERY route, also on Conditions), ACTIVE FIRES (`FireNearRoute`, a dropdown whose closed row names the fire count and the nearest), KNOWN HAZARDS (`SafetyMatrix`), cell coverage, EMERGENCY & RESCUE (handed in by `ClimbMatch.jsx` as `safetyPanels`), then the FLOAT PLAN (`FloatPlanFold`, a dropdown, on committing objectives only), then *Got beta?* and the discussion. `check:bare` holds forecast → fire → float plan.
- Safety tab: **EMERGENCY & RESCUE** (`EmergencyRescueCard`) names the COUNTY the climb is in (the route's `emergency.county`, else `useCountyAt` on `climbPoint`'s coordinate — the FCC census-area lookup, cached per ~100 m cell in localStorage) and shows only that county's verified non-emergency dispatch line from `SAR_BY_COUNTY` (`sarFor`); a county with no verified number shows 911 guidance and none. Its Edit pencil opens *Add or fix info* at the `emergency` field (`setAutoFix(id+"#emergency")`).
- Photos tab: route photos, lightbox, likes, report. **+ Add photo opens a sheet with ONE box, the picker** (owner, 2026-10-24): picked photos show as thumbnails and there is no per-photo describe box; their alt falls back to the route (`photoAlt`). **A topo photo shows only on the route page it was posted from** (`topos.route_id`, 0364; `useRouteTopos`): it used to be read by area, and an alpine route's area is the whole peak, so a photo of the far face showed on every route of the mountain. Insert requires `route_id`; the 3 topos posted before it record no route and show on no route page (rows kept). Saved-offline topos are filtered the same way (`offlineSavedTopos(areaId, routeId)`). Its `TopoDrawer` is exported for the log form (`lib/LogAscent.jsx`), so a topo can be drawn while logging a climb. It can be drawn from a keyboard too: the photo takes focus, arrow keys move a cross, Enter places a point, [ and ] select one (WCAG 2.1.1, 2026-10-23). A line can be named (`topo_lines.label`, optional); the name is the line's chip in `TopoPhotoModal` and a caption on `TopoThumb`. In the editor only a TAP marks: a press that moves more than `TAP_SLOP` pans the photo, two fingers (or the wheel, or − / + / Fit) zoom it (`clampTopoView`, `zoomTopoView`), so looking around the wall never draws. The route line is red (`TOPO_RED`) with a dark edge (`TopoStroke`); *Draw line* also draws yellow variations (`TOPO_VAR`, stored in `topo_lines.variants`, 0330) — there is no variant pin any more. *Add pins* has a **Grade** marker: a pin of category `grade` whose note is the grade, printed as white text beside the pitch (`GradeTag`), modelled on Steph Abegg's overlays; grade labels take no number (`pinNums`), so belays still count 1, 2, 3. Uploading a topo photo opens the editor straight away (with a caption box). In `TopoPhotoModal` a line you drew (`created_by` = you) shows *Edit your line*, which reopens `TopoDrawer` with that line and saves through `updateTopoLine`, and *Delete line* (`deleteTopoLine`); a photo you posted shows *Delete this photo* (`deleteTopoPhoto`) unless another climber's line is on it, as Mountain Project and 27 Crags keep a shared photo up. `TopoDrawer` compares the drawing with how it opened: Cancel, and through `dirtyRef` the sheet's ✕ and backdrop, ask *Discard this drawing?* before dropping unsaved work. Variations are also DASHED (`TopoStroke` `dashed`, `TopoSwatch`) and each takes an optional caption typed while it is the active line, stored in `topo_lines.variant_labels` (0362, aligned with `variants` by index); the enlarged topo tags each variation V / V1, V2… on its middle vertex (`VarTag`) and lists them with their captions above the pin key (`TopoVariantKey`). The editor's line dots are 7px (11 selected) inside a 28px grab box. Markers (`PIN_CATEGORIES`): belay, grade, **bolt** (×), rappel, natural anchor, **crux** (lightning), bivy, and **hazard**, drawn as a red warning triangle rather than a disc. **Captions**: the climber who posted a topo photo can write a caption with no length cap (`topos.caption`, 0362; `updateTopoCaption`, uploader-only by the 0026 policy) — offered in the draw sheet right after upload and as *+ Add a caption / Edit caption* in the viewer (`TopoCaptionBox`); everyone reads it under the enlarged topo (`TopoCaption`, folded behind Show more past ~5 lines) and its first two lines under the thumbnail. It is separate from the photo description (`alt`), which is screen-reader text capped at 300.

- **On the desktop website (1024px+)**: a **breadcrumb** of the climb's areas (state › … › crag/peak, each a link to that area) sits above the header; the header is **facts beside a photo** — the first photo any climber shared (click: Photos tab), an *Add the first photo* box when there is none, or *Photos aren't reachable* when the photo read failed. At **1280px+** the **route map** leaves the Overview and sits in the right rail (`#cm-route-rail`, a portal from `RouteDetail`) on every tab; below 1280 it stays where it is. Phone layout unchanged.

- **Print (desktop website)**: a *⎙ Print* button beside Share opens a one-sheet printout — area path, facts (grade, length, pitches, season, aspect, approach, gain), approach, pitch by pitch, descent, rack, hazards and waypoints with coordinates — through `printSheet`, built by `routeSheetHtml` (`lib/routeSheet.js`). It prints only what the route stores, in the climber's units. No map image (the waypoints table carries the coordinates).

- **Sharing a climb** (*↗ Share* in the header): **Send to a friend in ClimbMatch** lists your friends (and finds any climber by name); *Send* posts a chat from you into their Messages carrying the climb's link (`routeLink`, with the way in you picked), which the chat draws as *Open the climb ›* and opens in place (`chatText` in core; App's `openSharedLink`). **Send another way** is `ShareVia` (`src/components/ShareVia.tsx`): Text (`sms:`), Email (`mailto:`), Copy link and *More…* (the phone's share sheet). The link opens the climb for anyone, with or without an account: a visitor on the sign-in screen is told a climb was shared (`arrival`, `lib/AuthModal.jsx`) and it opens after sign-in or *View as guest*. The link is also kept on the device (`SHARED_LINK_KEY`, `readSharedStash` in `ClimbMatch.jsx`) until the visitor is in, and App re-opens it then with an *Opening the climb that was shared with you…* toast: a read still in flight when they tap *View as guest*, one that failed under the sign-in screen, or a sign-in that leaves the page (Google, Apple, an emailed confirmation) used to drop them on Home. Kept a day at most. **Save as PDF** (phones too, not only the desktop *⎙ Print*) opens `routeSheetHtml` in a new tab, so the app stays where it was; the sheet's *← Back to ClimbMatch* closes that tab. Every shared link starts from `appLink` in core, which reads `VITE_PUBLIC_URL` when the website has its own address (unset today: the Pages address).

- **Draw approach (desktop website)**: *✎ Draw approach* in the header opens a map (`DrawRouteView`, `lib/DrawRoute.jsx`, on `TrailDrawMap`) to click the way in from the trailhead (T) toward the summit (S) or base (B) — a crag keeps P and C. It shows the line's length and the gain and loss read from terrain heights (Open-Meteo elevation, the forecast provider), and downloads it as a GPX whose description says it is hand-drawn, not recorded. On a crag, *Suggest this as the trail* opens the reviewed trail submission (`GpsSubmissionModal`, `initialPts`) with the line already drawn; elsewhere that form takes the climb's own track, so no suggestion is offered.
- **Compare (desktop website)**: *+ Compare* beside Print adds the climb to a tray pinned at the bottom of the window (up to four); *Compare N* opens a side-by-side table — grade, discipline, where, length, pitches, approach, gain, aspect, season, rating, hazards and the best day this week (the conditions score, crag disciplines only; alpine says it is judged by flags). Every cell is a stored value or "—" (`CompareTray`, `CompareView` in `lib/CompareRoutes.jsx`).

## 2. How it works

1. **Open.** `openRoute(x)` in `ClimbMatch.jsx` sets `routeFrom`, `selRoute` and `tab="routes"`. `App` renders `RouteDetail` (a lazy screen).
2. **Load (DB path, `USE_DB`).** `useAreaRoutes` (lib/db.js) backs the area browser's route list from `areas`/`routes`; `fetchRouteRow` fetches a single full row. `dbRouteToCamel()` converts a snake_case row to the camelCase shape the page reads. Seed `ROUTES` are only the fallback/demo set.
3. **Overlay of climber input.** `App` merges contributions onto the route inside its route memo: `useRouteContributions` supplies the route's contribution rows; only `status='approved'` rows are applied (value = `applied_value` else `value`), producing `_contribFields` (approved fields), `_pendingEdits` (the caller's OWN pending suggestions, shown as "Awaiting review") and `_contribAgreed` (how many climbers submitted the approved value; drives "✓ N climbers, checked" in the edit form). `useRouteDifficultyRatings` feeds the difficulty breakdown.
4. **Stars.** `useMyRouteRating(routeId, userId)` reads your `route_ratings` row into `myStars` (a failed read never clears the stars). `setRouteRating(userId, routeId, stars)` upserts, or deletes when `stars` is null, then invalidates `route-rating-mine` and `route-star-summary`. `useRouteStarSummary(routeId)` calls RPC `route_star_summary` for the shared average and rater count shown in the ★ cell.
5. **Sub-tabs** are rendered by `RouteDetail.jsx` off its `tab` state. The approach picker (`ApproachPicker`, lib/approaches.js) is the first card UNDER the sub-tab bar, on every tab, because the pick drives every tab; with more than one way in it is a radio group headed *Choose your approach · N ways in*. *Other finishes from this route* (routes reached BY this one) is a separate card, on Overview only. A **variation** — a climb that leaves its via route partway and rejoins it, like Boving Roofs on the Southwest Rib — records the via route's pitches it also climbs as `viaPitches` (`{before:[1,3], after:[6,7]}`) on that way in: Overview then lists **The whole climb · N pitches** in climbing order (`wholeLine`), the via route's pitches read live from its own row; the Plan tab's time note names those pitches; the variation keeps its OWN trip plan instead of the via route's; and the parent's card reads *Variations of this route*, each chip saying where it leaves and rejoins (`viaPitchSpans`). Overview carries the consensus (`buildConsensus` over `route.activity`), hazard flags, route breakdown, approach/descent, social chips (`useRoutePairings`, `useRouteLoggedWith`). `FireNearRoute` is the first section of Safety on every route. A start of climb is filed from the *Start of climb* section of Add or fix info (or the log form) and shown as START OF CLIMB on Overview; Safety has no start-location button (removed 2026-10-15 as a duplicate). On a crag route (`cragOnly`) the top card is ROUTE FACTS — the number tiles, composite grade, aliases and first ascents — and the guidebook facts are three sections further down, built by `TechStats` with a `part` and drawn by `CragFactSection` (`CRAG_FACT_SECTIONS`): **LOCATION** (location, start) above GETTING THERE, then **GEAR & SAFETY** (bolts, protection, anchor, rope, fixed gear, landing, pads, descent) and **CONDITIONS** (aspect, sun, season, wet, rock quality) right under the RACK card. Facts on file come first; each missing one reads *Not on file yet* with its own **+ Add**, which opens the editor at that field (`FACT_FIELD`).
6. **Planner.** Time estimates come from `scarfHrs(...)` (Naismith-style approach by fitness tier and pack weight) and `techHrs(...)` (pitch-by-pitch climbing, exponential slowdown by grade); `lib/planTimes.js` (`planTimes`, `realPitches`) assembles the stage totals. A route's stored legs win over computed ones (`check:return-leg`). Each day of the trip plan shows its gain, loss, hours, miles and pack weight as ONE row of pills that share its width, so the pack weight no longer wraps onto a line of its own on a phone.
   **CAMPING & BIVY** (`CampingPanel`, under the calculator) merges `route.bivy` with Campsite pins (`campSites`): a pin and a site are one camp on an exact name, or under `samePlaceCamp` (both carry a height within 150 ft and the shorter name's distinctive words are all in the longer; upper/lower/middle count), and the pin lends its trail distance. The sites are ONE list **in the order the walk in reaches them** (`campRouteOrder`, applied inside `campSites`, so the log form's *Where did you camp?* reads the same order): by trail distance from the trailhead, a site with no distance slotted in by its height, a site with neither last in stored order; the intro says so (`campOrderNote`). What a site is for is a tag on its card (`campRoleLabel`: *Where most parties sleep* / *Other option*, none when unsorted), not a group heading, and a card whose distance and the one above's are both known says *N mi on from <previous camp> · N ft higher* (`campFromPrev`). To help a party choose, each card can carry a green *Night N of the D-day trip plan* tag (`campNightsOf` reads only where an itinerary day ENDS: after the title's last "to", an arrival objective, or "return to") and *≈N h walk in at your planner pace* (`campWalkHrs`: `scarfHrs` over the camp's distance and gain at the fitness and pack set in the calculator, null without both). Each card also says *Slept here in N trip reports, latest <date>* from the log form's *Where did you camp?* (`campReportsOf` over the merged `activity`, matching a report's `cond.camps` to a site by NAME, case aside, nothing fuzzier; private reports count anonymously through `route_private_conditions`), and camps that reports name but the list lacks render under *NAMED IN TRIP REPORTS · NOT LISTED YET* with an *Add a camp* button that opens the same editor (nothing a report typed is written into the route). Guarded by `check:camping`. Its pencil (and *Contribute*) opens the **Camping & bivy** field of the edit sheet, filed under the group heading *Approach, descent & camping*: the row names the camps on file (`campNames`), every box in a site carries a visible caption, and a stored height is shown in the reader's units (`_bivyElevBox`) because the box is converted back to feet on save. Where `campingGate(route)` is false (a crag line that climbs no peak) the sheet drops the field and the heading reads *Approach & descent*, since no screen would show a camp filed there.
7. **Glacier terrain.** Alpine routes whose terrain read says glacier get the glacier conditions card plus the rock-weather flags (#2336).
8. **Photos.** `submitRoutePhoto` writes a `contributions` row of kind `photo`; `reportPhoto` reports one (into `content_reports`; there is no in-app queue since 2026-10-24 — read it in the Supabase dashboard); `lib/photoPrivacy.js` / `lib/photoEvidence.js` support gating.
9. **Suggest a fix on KNOWN HAZARDS (Safety).** `SafetyMatrix`'s header *✎ Edit* (the compact `EditIconButton` every route-page card header uses; its name is *Suggest a fix to the known hazards*) opens `SuggestFix` on its Hazards field with nothing picked; a *Fixed gear* chip opens it with that one hazard already picked (an extra chip beside the stock options). Hazards is a multi field, so the seed is always an ARRAY of strings — a bare string, or the click event the button once passed through, crashed the page.
10. **Comments are live.** `useComments` (lib/db.js) is a `useLiveQuery` (`lib/live.js`): it subscribes over Supabase Realtime to `comments` and `comment_reactions`, and a row another climber inserts, edits, deletes or reacts to on any target this page holds (the route, its sub-tabs, pitches, photos, an area, a group post) refetches the comments, so the thread fills in without a reload (0326 put the tables in the `supabase_realtime` publication). The refetch goes through the same query and RLS as a reload.

## 4. Data

- **`routes`** (thousands of rows; `dbRouteToCamel` reads it). `routes.stars` is a cache kept by triggers on `route_ratings` and `climb_logs`; the Climbs list, min-stars filter and stars sort read it.
- **`route_ratings`** (0294): PK `(route_id, user_id)`, `stars` 1-5, RLS own-row read/insert/update/delete. Nothing renders who rated what.
- **`route_private_conditions(p_route)`** (0329, `useRoutePrivateConditions`): every report on the route the caller cannot read in full (kept to friends, a crew or the climber alone), as conditions only: date, condition/hazard tags, outcome and its reasons, times, snow/water/bugs/trail/protection/crowd answers, and an opaque per-route `reporter_key`. No author, text, photos, beta, partners or track; taken-down and blocked-either-way rows excluded. `anonRowToActivity` (`lib/tripReportRow.js`) marks them `_anon`; RouteDetail feeds them to the Overview consensus, `ConsensusPanel` and `AlpineConditionsCard` only (`condActivity`), never to a list of reports. This is Mountain Project's private-tick model: a private tick still counts on the route page without a name.
- **`route_views`** (0353): PK `(route_id, visitor, day)`, `visitor` an md5, RLS on with no policies — no client reads or writes a row. **`record_route_view(p_route, p_device)`** inserts one (ignored for an unknown route or a device key outside 16–64 characters); **`route_stats(p_route)`** returns one jsonb of counts: `views_total`, `views_30d`, `views_since`, `climbs`, `climbers`, `sends`, `senders`, `last_climbed`, `objectives`, `star_counts` (1★…5★), `sends_by_month` (Jan…Dec). Both callable signed out.
- **`route_star_summary(p_route_id)`**: average of one vote per climber: header rating, else their most recent public, visible trip report's stars; returns `stars` and `raters`.
- **`route_base_checkins`** (0188): one row per climber per route, written only through `check_in_at_route_base()`; others read unattributed SPOTS through `route_base_spots()` (0363; `useRouteBaseCheckins`, read once in `RouteDetail` and shared by the map and the panel; `route_base_checkin_points()` still returns raw points). A trigger feeds a confirmed spot into `routes.waypoints`' Base pin; **`route_base_pin_changes`** (owner-only, RLS with no policies) logs every placement, move and hold.
- **`contributions`**: corrections (held `pending` until the owner approves; 0307–0309) and route photos (kind `photo`). **`climb_logs`**: trip reports/stars.
- **Still seed/in-memory:** `ROUTES` fallback set and demo trip reports when not on the DB path.

## 8. Known gaps / open decisions

- Live comments: Realtime applies RLS to an UPDATE, so a comment a moderator takes down is never SENT to other readers; it leaves their screen at the next refetch (refocus, reload, a write), not the instant it is removed.

- `SAR_BY_COUNTY` holds 3 Utah counties (Grand, Utah, Salt Lake), each checked against the agency's own page on 2026-10-15; every other county shows 911 guidance only until a climber adds its line through the pencil. Emergency contacts are per ROUTE, so a climber's edit on one Castleton line does not reach its neighbours.

- A base check-in at a CRAG is judged against the crag's coordinate (500 m), so the server cannot tell which of two neighbouring lines a climber stood under; a spot is trustworthy only as far as several climbers agree on it. The find-the-base readout says so by handing over to the `location` note inside GPS error.

- #2403 states the corrections merge sits inside App's route memo and was not exercised against live contributions; only build guards cover it. It also records a database-path gap in `docs/guards/contributions-and-logbook.md`.
- #2336 did not run `check:ui` or `check:overflow` after the Total row grew from 3 to 4 tiles.
- Only 5 of 314,599 routes held a `stars` value when 0294 was written; a route's value is replaced only when a climber votes (per migration comment).
