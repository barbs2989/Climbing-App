# The route page

Part of the current-state docs. The Conditions tab has its own doc: [conditions.md](conditions.md).

## 1. What it does today

- Tapping a climb anywhere in the app opens its page (`tab="routes"` with `selRoute` set). The header carries the five rating stars with the vote count beside them ("N votes", or "No votes"; Mountain Project's "from N votes", AllTrails' "(N)"), a ★ cell with the average over "★ N votes" (the same count), grade, discipline and save/log actions. The discipline badges come from `rDiscs` (via `DiscBadges`): the route's primary (`catOf`) first, then the other disciplines its `disciplines` list names, never "Scrambling" on a route with a 5.x grade.
- **Section cards share one padding** (owner note 2026-10-25, box audit): every route-page section card is 12px × 14px with a 12px radius, crag and alpine alike (the crag cards were 13px × 15px). Tiles and tappable rows keep their own padding, on purpose.
- **Jump links** (`SectionJumps` in `src/components/SectionJumps.tsx`): a pill row at the top of every sub-tab, one pill per section heading (4+ only). A tap jumps instantly and flashes the heading; the heading lands 12px **below** the sticky app bar, measured at tap time, never under it (2026-10-21: a fixed 12px margin hid it behind the 59px bar). A pill names the heading's TITLE only: a fold heading that wraps another heading is skipped for the inner one, and a fold whose title sits beside a summary marks the title `data-jump-label` (2026-10-28: Safety read "Active fire nearby4 fires" and a second "FLOAT PLANCommitting objectiv…" pill).
- **Section headings carry the map legend's icon** where the whole section is one waypoint type: CAMPING & BIVY the purple ⌂ (Campsite), APPROACH the ⇢ (Approach), GETTING THERE the green ◈ (Trailhead). `SL`'s `wp` prop draws `wpGlyph`/`wpColor` from `WP_STYLE`, the same table the route map's legend and pins use, so they cannot drift. Mixed sections (WAYPOINTS, DESCENT) take none.
- **The twelve waypoint colours are spread for distance, not just hue** (`WP_STYLE`): the seven familiar ones (green trailhead, yellow junction, blue water, purple campsite, orange summit, red hazard) plus a pale sky Topout, aqua Base, pale stone Crag, dark olive Pass, chartreuse Approach and pink Landmark. The closest pair is 19.3 in CIEDE2000; `check:wp-styles` fails any pair under 18. A recorded pin's glyph takes white or near-black, whichever contrasts more with its fill (`wpInk`).
- Sub-tabs: `overview`, `planner`, `forecast` (labelled "Conditions"), `conditions` ("Reports" / "Send Reports"), `safety`, `partners`, `photos`. **Crag routes have no Plan tab** (see `CRAG_CATS`, `showPlan`); the Safety tab is unconditional. The sub-tab bar scrolls sideways, and on a phone its last tabs (Partners, Photos) sit past the right edge; while a tab is hidden off either end, that edge fades out and carries a round **‹ / ›** button that scrolls the bar (`tabEdge`, `nudgeTabs`, 2026-10-23 — the MUI scrollable-tabs / Notion pattern). A bar that fits shows neither.
- Your header stars persist. Tapping another star changes your rating; tapping your own star again withdraws it. Signed out (DB builds), a tap lights nothing: it says "Sign in to rate this climb" and opens sign-in. It used to light the stars while saving nothing, so the ★ cell and the count below never moved and the page contradicted itself. Signed in, the write refreshes `route-star-summary`, so the ★ cell and both counts move as soon as it saves. There is no minimum-votes filter on the Climbs list: Mountain Project's route finder filters by minimum stars only, and the list keeps to its set.
- Every figure built from several climbers' input states how many climbers it comes from and over what window (e.g. "avg of N ratings", "N climbers logged it", "N in the last 45 days", "N of 3 suggested").
- **Every dropdown is drawn 44px tall** (owner note 2026-10-29, "On route beta, it's difficult to click the dropdown"): ROUTE BREAKDOWN's rows (`RouteBreakdown`) are at least 44px with their content centred (they were 35-38px, stacked 9px apart), and so are RAPPELS' and CAMPING & BIVY's rows, ACCESS & REGULATIONS' header, KNOWN HAZARDS' report rows, a recorded track's row, the route tags (`TagChip`: a 26px chip inside a transparent 44px control), *Show more* (`Capped`), *Read more* / *Show more* on comments and captions, *Show N earlier comments*, the hourly forecast's toggle and *Your conditions*. ROUTE BREAKDOWN's *Tap for detail* was a filled blue chip that looked like a button and did nothing; it is a plain caption, *Tap a row for detail*. The rest of the app's dropdowns got the same (Home, Groups, Crews, Calendar, lists, the area tree's expanders). Guard: `check:dropdown-targets`.
- **Climbers who logged a route are asked for belay stations** (2026-10-28, multipitch audit proposal 2): ROUTE BREAKDOWN shows a blue *You’ve climbed this* box (`data-belay-ask`) to anyone who has logged the route, when any pitch still has no belay station: how many lack one, and *Add belay stations*, which opens the pitch editor at the first of them (`RouteBreakdown`’s `logged` prop). Online research filled 449 pitches; the rest can only come from climbers.
- **Belay station per pitch** (owner note 2026-10-25): a pitch row has a `belay` key, the belay station (where the belayer stands and how to find it). It is typed in the pitch editor (*Pitch N belay station*), shown first in the *Pitch gear* card as *Belay station*, and checked by `enrich-approach-rappel-bivy.mjs` for source names like the other pitch text. Researched 2026-10-28: 449 pitches on 162 of the 424 routes with pitch detail now carry one, each verified against a source and written by `scripts/oneoff/apply-belay-stations.mjs` (method, batches and rollbacks in `audits/belay-stations/`). A pitch no source locates says so in the card.
- **ROUTE STATS** (`RouteStats`, `lib/RouteStats.jsx`, 0353; DB path only): a card at the foot of Overview, just above *Quick log* / *Full log*, modelled on Mountain Project's sidebar and Stats page and theCrag's season chart. Four tiles: **Page views** (total, and how many in the last 30 days), **Sends** (*Sends / summits* off crags; by N climbers, and how many of them in the past 12 months), **Logged climbs** (by N climbers, how many in the past 12 months — 0371, a trailing 365 days so October still counts the summer season — and the date of the last one) and **On objectives** (climbers who saved it; a button, `onOpenPartners`, that opens the Partners sub-tab, as Mountain Project's Stats page lists who has a route on a to-do list. It reads *See who* when anyone has saved it, and *Partners at this crag* on a crag route, whose partners are found by area. Owner note, 2026-10-28); then the **star breakdown** (5★ to 1★, the same votes as the header's "N votes") and **sends by month** (Jan–Dec bars over dated sends). A view is one visitor, one route, one UTC day: `recordRouteView` runs once when the page opens, on any sub-tab; the server takes a signed-in viewer from `auth.uid()` and a signed-out one from a random key kept on the device, and stores only an md5 of it. Private logs count as numbers with no name (Mountain Project's private-tick model, as in 0329). A send is what Ranks calls one (not Attempt, Turned around, Fell, Hung, Project or Conditions); a Conditions report is not a logged climb. A failed read says *Route stats aren't reachable right now*, never zeros. The chip strip under the header still counts only the reports you can read, because it opens them.
- **Long terrain text, the month-by-month link, and road notes out of Trip plan** (owner note 2026-10-28, Mount Logan): in ROUTE BREAKDOWN a travel leg's terrain longer than 18 characters or three words ("Hiking, steep descent through alpine meadows and forest") is a small full-width line under the leg's name (`RouteBreakdown`, `longTerrain`), not a chip wrapping beside the label or a third-width bold tile; a short one stays a chip. Trip plan's italic "Full month-by-month seasonal guidance is further down this tab." is now a *Month by month* chip beside *Typical season* that goes to SEASONAL GUIDANCE (`ItineraryView`'s `onSeeSeason`; *· Conditions tab* when the panel lives there, which is every route with a Conditions tab, where the old sentence was false). An itinerary's `cal` note no longer carries road or closure text: those sentences were moved into the route's `road.seasonalGate` (GETTING THERE's *Road status*) or `access.closures`, or dropped where that text already said them, by `scripts/oneoff/move-cal-road-notes.mjs`; what stays in `cal` (permits, season, snow on the route) is still drawn in Trip plan.
- **Pitch detail, part 1** (owner note 2026-10-25): in ROUTE BREAKDOWN a pitch shows *Pitch gear* with the anchor and the gear for it each in a full-width box (`PitchConsensus`) and an *Edit belay, anchor & gear* button that opens that pitch in the editor; Length and Bolts stay as tiles. Each pitch has *Photos* (`PitchPhotos`): *Add a photo of this pitch* files a route photo tagged with the pitch (`submitRoutePhoto`'s `pitch`, read back as `_pitch`), and the pitch lists its own. Pitch comments (`PitchComments`) show the newest three with *Show N earlier comments*; a comment over 240 characters clamps with *Read more* (`ClampText`).
- **BETA leads to the climb, and every ROUTE BREAKDOWN box opens** (owner note 2026-10-22): the Overview BETA card ends in *Follow the climb section by section*, naming the section and its count (*Route breakdown · 5 sections · on the Plan tab*); it switches to Plan (crag routes stay on Overview) and scrolls to CLIMBING ROUTE (`#climbing-route`) when one is written, else to ROUTE BREAKDOWN (`#route-breakdown`). Shown only when that section has rows. In `RouteBreakdown` every row opens: a travel leg (gully, rappel, snowfield) gets *✎ Edit this section*, only the Terrain / Length / Anchor tiles that hold a value, and *Comments on this section* (`PitchComments`, `title` prop). *Edit this pitch/section* passes the row's `pitchDetail` index and the editor finds the row by `data-src`. It used to pass the pitch-only count, which on a mixed route opened the wrong row. Mountain Project's topos tie each pitch's beta to that pitch the same way. A roped pitch drops an empty Length / Bolts tile the same way a leg does (2026-10-29), so an open box no longer shows a row of dashes; PitchConsensus still asks for what is missing. `Capped` (the 620px fold every route section mounts in) marks itself open on any tap inside a section that has not folded yet: opening a ROUTE BREAKDOWN box used to push the section past the cap, and it folded and hid the box just tapped (Big Four Tower Route, P2). Thin boxes on 61 routes were researched on 2026-10-29 (`audits/box-detail-2026-10-29/`): 19 boxes on 8 routes written, 13 single-source routes held.
- **Plan tab headers** (owner's note, 2026-10-25): Trip plan, *My itinerary*, ROUTE BREAKDOWN, RAPPELS and CAMPING & BIVY are all `SL` section headers. *My itinerary* was a `CardHead` card title and now matches them. CAMPING & BIVY alone carries its map-legend glyph (⌂), kept on purpose (see the section-heading note above). *Full trip time* joins them (2026-10-28): it was a 14px blue title inside its own card, and is now an `SL` header above the card.
- **Pitch comments need an account** (2026-10-28): `PitchComments` shows its comment box only when it gets an `onAdd`. The route page passes none when the database is on and nobody is signed in (`commentApi.canPost`), so a guest reads *Sign in to add a comment* instead. A guest's comment used to be kept in that tab only, shown as posted and lost on refresh. The pitch's Photos box already said the same.
- Planner (alpine layout) has a **Full trip time** card: Approach, Climbing, Descent, Total trip (approach + climb + descent). **Rest and breaks** (owner, 2026-10-26): `lib/planTimes.js` adds `REST_PER_MOVING_HR` (1/6 = 10 min per hour of moving time) to every MODELLED approach, descent walk and climb, not to pitch belay changes and not to stored published legs. Basis: DNT 12 min/h, Bergfreunde 5–10, Walkhighlands 10, RMI and Northwest Alpine Guides 10 min each hour; these are guide-service and forum sources, not a standards body. Calibration against logged trip-report durations is still open. **A stored approach or descent with no summit leg is a floor** (2026-10-28): `planTimes` used to drop it and walk the leg from distance and gain, which came out more than 20% short on 20 of the 69 WA routes that store an approach this way (0 hr on 6 with no walk inputs; Davis Peak's North Face read no estimate at all). Now the leg is the longer of the walk model and the stored figure (`loneApproachH` / `loneDescentH`), so it can only lengthen, and the Planner shows an estimate when those are the only times on file. 27 WA routes got longer and none shorter; `check:return-leg` holds it.
- **ACCESS & REGULATIONS is a dropdown, closed by default** (`AccessFold`; its toggle is a transparent button stretched behind the header row, so the confidence chip's Flag is never a button inside a button): one row shows the heading and the labels of what it holds (e.g. "Permit · Fees · Land manager"); tapping it opens the permit, fees, closures, rules, land manager, pass, parking and permit links. The pencil stays beside it. The body is hidden, not unmounted, so the text stays in the markup. It sits `FOLD_GAP` (24px, twice `ROUTE_GAP`) below the card above it, so the closed row does not read as part of the trailhead cards; the Safety tab's two dropdowns (`FireNearRoute`, `FloatPlanFold`) take the same gap above and below.
- Plan tab, GETTING THERE: under the `TrailheadCard` (name, drive directions, then the copyable coordinates on their own full-width row and *Google Maps* / *Apple Maps* to the same point as two equal 44px buttons beneath — at 390px three controls on one line cut the coordinate to "48.85…", owner note 2026-10-25) sits `SummitLegsCard` — **Trailhead to summit** (gain, distance) and **Summit to trailhead** (distance), the trip-profile layout of The Mountaineers' route pages. Each leg carries its **time** (`Nhr`, the Full Trip Time card's format): *Trailhead to summit* is the time up (approach + climb, `totalH`) and *Summit to trailhead* the time down (`downH`). Both come from `planTimes(route, calc)` with the pace, pack, party and departure set in Full trip time (`calcInputs`, passed in as `calc`), so the card, the **DESCENT** section's leg strip (`DescentLegStrip`, the same geometry from `tripLegGeometry`) and Full trip time are one estimate and cannot disagree (owner's trip-profile note, 2026-10-25; this supersedes the 2026-10-26 "no times" rule, which existed only because the card then ran at the party's defaults). Gain only where it is the whole climb (alpine/mountaineering/hiking, or gain ≈ loss); per-leg distance only on an out-and-back (a loop or point-to-point gives the whole outing once); a whole-day-only stored time renders one **Car to car** row. Never on a crag route. `check:trailhead-directions` section 9.
- Plan tab, **TRIP PROFILE** (owner's note, 2026-10-25: "each leg of the route in the plan section detailed on timing ... it must match up among sections"), directly above Full trip time: `TripProfileCard` prints one row per leg between the route's OWN waypoints, **UP** (trailhead first) then **DOWN** (the same legs walked back), each with its distance, its gain or drop, and its time, and a *Whole approach and climb* / *Whole descent* row. `lib/tripProfile.js` (`tripProfile`, `apportion`) does the work. A waypoint snaps to its nearest vertex of the recorded track (within `PROFILE_SNAP_M`, 300 m, the track caveats' own tolerance); the track between two snapped vertices is a leg. Its distance is measured along the track; its climb is the track's own rises when the track carries elevations (`climbBy: "track"`), else the height change between the two pins when both store one (`"pins"`), else no per-leg gain or drop is printed. The legs are a SPLIT of the route's own totals, not separate numbers: distance by the track, climb by the same measurement, time by `scarfHrs()` leg by leg, so the legs sum to `GETTING THERE` (Trailhead to summit, Summit to trailhead) and to the DESCENT strip exactly, at one rounding (`apportion`, largest remainder). A route with no profile renders nothing: no recorded track, a stub or placeholder track, no trailhead or summit pin on the track, a car-to-car day, or a loop or point-to-point (one whole-outing number, no split). Pins off the track have no leg of their own, and the card says how many.
- Plan tab, THE CLIMB, in a climber's order: APPROACH → **CLIMBING ROUTE** (`ClimbingRouteTable`, the climb at a glance, section by section) → **ROUTE BREAKDOWN** (`RouteBreakdown`, every pitch and leg) → DESCENT → rappels. Overview before detail, as guidebooks print a route (owner, 2026-10-22). CLIMBING ROUTE renders wherever `climbing_route` is written, pitched routes included (it was hidden on the 46 alpine routes that carry one, and drawn AFTER the breakdown on 121 scramble/mountaineering routes); its empty *Describe the climbing* state stays unpitched-only. When both are on the page, `BreakdownHandoff` joins them: a short spine under the overview and "↓ Pitch by pitch below — N roped pitches and M legs in the route breakdown.", counted by `breakdownRows()`. No section is matched to a pitch: nothing stored says which pitches a section spans.
- RAPPELS (`RappelTable`): **each rappel is a dropdown, like a camp under CAMPING & BIVY** (owner note 2026-10-24, to save screen space). `RappelRow` always shows R#, length and anchor, plus a red *N hazards* count when the station has any; *▸ More* opens finding the station, hazards, the pull and notes. The first rappel opens expanded, as the first camp does. A row with none of those has no toggle.
- Climbers can edit/correct route fields through edit pencils; every edit is held until the owner approves it (see [route-quality-control.md](route-quality-control.md)).
- Crag routes show `CragLocationCard` (crag + parking coordinates, Google/Apple Maps). Its *Add parking*, *Add the pin* and the pencil on a parking pin open the `waypoints` pin editor (map picker + lat/lng). That editor is offered on every crag discipline. On a crag it is labelled *Parking spot* and its only type is the parking pin (`WP_TYPES_CRAG`; a crag climb carries no waypoints), and the stored `Trailhead` type is shown as *Parking* (`wpTypeLbl`). `trailheadPoint()` reads that pin before the crag-wide `areas.parking_*` pin.
- **A class range is one grade plus a notice.** A stored range such as "Class 3-4" or "3rd-4th class" headlines its top (`displayGrade` / `cruxGrade` in `lib/grade.js`), which is the grade `grade_num` and the finder filter already use; each Composite Grade chip shows one grade (`singleGrade`: the route's headline class when the range contains it, else the top), and `gradeRangeNote` writes the spread under the chips: *Graded Class 4 for its hardest moves; easier sections down to Class 3*, or, when the headline is below the range top, *Graded Class 3 on its standard line; some sections or variations reach Class 4*. Only the head of a value counts; a range inside its qualifier stays prose. **Ranges on the other scales take the same rule** (owner note, 2026-10-29): "V4-5", "WI3-4", "M5-6", "A2-3", "C0-1" and "5.2-5.4" (~15,700 rows, mostly imported boulders and ice) show their top grade in the header, list rows, chips, Crux grade tile and an area's *Rock difficulty* span (`topGrade`), and `gradeRangeNote` adds *Rated V4-5; shown at the harder of the two grades, V5.* That notice claims nothing about how the problem is split, because such a range means a grade between the two, not a mix of V4 and V5 sections. A slashed letter ("5.10a/b") is a single YDS grade and is left alone. **A slashed number is a range** (2026-10-30): "V1/2", "WI3/4", "5.9/10" and "5.11/5.12" headline their top and get the same notice, and `gradeNumFrom` now files them at the top as well (it read "V1/2" as V1 and "5.9/10" as 5.9, so they sorted and filtered at the lower grade); so does the client filter `routeBandIdx`. A YDS slash counts only when its second number is within two of the first and no word follows it, so "5.9 / 15m" stays a 5.9. Free-or-aid alternatives such as "5.8 A2 or 5.10" are two ways to climb it, not a range, and keep both.
- On **sport, trad and top-rope** routes the grade is shown once, in the Composite Grade panel of ROUTE FACTS / TECHNICAL STATS (`TechStats`). The *Crux grade* tile appears beside it only when a stored crux grade differs from the grade that panel shows; every other discipline keeps the tile as before.
- **Climb length on alpine and mountaineering routes** comes from `length_m` (shown as *Climb length* in TECHNICAL STATS). It was blank on 5,136 of 5,669 such routes; `scripts/pipeline/fill-length-from-mp.mjs` fills it from the Mountain Project exports already on disk (`MP_DIR`), with the importer's own conversion (feet ÷ 3.28084), only where `length_m` is blank and only from the import's exact MP-id map. Routes whose MP ids disagree are listed, not written, and no figure is estimated. Gain, distance and high point have no structured route-level source the rules accept (the sites that print them refuse automated reads), so they stay blank where nobody has entered them. Re-run the script after any import; filled rows are skipped.
- **High point on alpine and mountaineering routes** comes from `high_point_ft` (*High point* in TECHNICAL STATS). `scripts/pipeline/fill-high-point-from-summit.mjs` fills a blank one only on a route that finishes on a summit, from the route's own Summit waypoint elevation, and only where it measures that source first: on the rows that already hold a value, the Summit pin reproduces it within 50 ft on 361 of 362 (99.7%), so it writes; the parent peak's `elevation_ft`, on rows with no pin, reproduces it on only 28 of 45 (62%), because routes on a peak's area often top out on a sub-summit or spire, so it does not. It refuses crag parents (there `high_point_ft` tracks the base), traverses, routes with base or topout pins, and routes whose name or text says they stop below the top. Run on 2026-10-28, it filled 6 routes; the other ~4,990 blanks have a crag parent or no summit elevation at all, and stay blank. Writes are compare-and-set into a NULL column only.
- **Gain and distance are not derived from stored GPX tracks.** Measured 2026-10-28 on the 208 alpine and mountaineering routes with a recorded, full-coverage track and a stored `dist_km`: the track length (halved when the track returns to its start) matches the stored figure within 10% on only 55. Stored tracks are one-way, round trip, loops or partial, and `dist_km` itself holds two conventions (see `docs/codebase/route-identity.md`). Climbing gain from `elev_pts` matched stored `gain_ft` within 10% on 26 of 38. Neither is good enough to write.
- **A scramble shows no pitch count** (owner note, 2026-10-25): a scrambling row's stored `pitches` is not printed in TECHNICAL STATS (`TechStats`), the list row beside the discipline (`DbAreaBrowser`), Compare or the printed sheet (`routeSheet.js`), because a scramble is never roped. Its climbing sections (CLIMBING ROUTE, ROUTE BREAKDOWN) stay on Plan. Seven catalog rows still store a count; they are listed for owner review.
- **The header's place line is the place's own name, not capitals** (owner's note 2026-10-24, "MOUNT INDEX"): the line above the climb's name prints `📍 Mount Index` as the area page titles it, not `MOUNT INDEX`. A proper name in forced capitals read as a section label and disagreed with the area page, the breadcrumb and every row naming the same place; Mountain Project, theCrag and AllTrails all print the location over a climb or trail in its own case.
- **An approach card shows its whole window, at the top** (owner note, 2026-10-25): `approach_variants[].season` sits in a full-width box at the top of each APPROACH card (`data-approach-window`), wrapping and never cut; the name and the facts follow it. It used to be a 48-character nowrap pill beside the name, cut with an ellipsis, with the full sentence repeated below. Route and name text in the approach list, the prev/next route buttons and the field-value rows wrap instead of truncating.
- **Most used, and why go another way** (owner note, 2026-10-28): with several ways in, the one marked `primary:true` is badged *Most used* on its APPROACH card and in the HOW YOU GET THERE picker. Each other way in may carry `approach_variants[].whyThisWay`, one sentence on when a party takes it instead ("early season, while the gully still holds snow"): it renders as *Why go this way* under that card's name (`data-approach-why`) and, for the picked way in, under the picker's trailhead line. It never renders on the most used card, nor on a route with no most used way in (there is nothing to be an alternative TO). Editable in the way-in editor (a box on each non-primary way in). Written in research batches by `scripts/apply-approach-why.mjs` (exact way-in names, no sources, no ALL CAPS, no 8-word runs of its sources, re-read after each write); census `scripts/census-approach-why.mjs`; evidence and rollbacks in `audits/approach-why/`. Guarded by `check:approach-section` §3c.
- **The header strip's season is a short window in one spelling**: `seasonShort()` (lib/seasonText.js) first runs `seasonTidy()` — a month pair is cut to three letters joined by a plain hyphen, a qualifier is its own word, the first letter is a capital (`"Mid-July to September"` → `"Mid Jul-Sep"`) — and skips a window its own clause negates (`"avoid May-Sep"` is never shown as the season). The Approaches pill uses the same function at 48 characters. The prose under the season chip and under an approach pill compares against `seasonTidy()` of the full text, so a respelled window does not print twice.
- **A route with no season of its own shows its area's** (owner note "Audit optimal season for all route via online research", 2026-10-30): 314k of 315k routes have no `routes.season`, and per-route research finds no source for one, while a crag's or peak's season is published once. `routeSeasonShort()` (lib/seasonText.js) feeds the header strip: the route's own `season` first, else its area's `areas.season_months`, else the parent area's (a wall or sector inherits its crag's), printed through `monthsLabel()` in the strip's hyphen spelling. An ice or mixed route never inherits (an area's season is researched for its rock; `scripts/oneoff/season-disciplines.mjs` checks each batch's areas are rock-dominant). The route reads embed `parent:parent_id(name,season_months)` (lib/offline.js `ROUTE_AREA_EMBED`, the two copies in lib/db.js, and the offline `areaEmbed`), mapped to `_dbArea.parentSeasonMonths`. The area values are researched by `scripts/enrich/area-page-apply.mjs` batches.
- **Every approach distance says which distance it is.** `dist_km` is the walk in, ONE WAY (a recorded loop or point-to-point holds the whole outing, `effDistIsWholeTrip`). The header strip prints the same figure and label as TechStats' tile (`headlineDist`: *Round trip* = doubled, *Total distance*, or plain *Distance* for an unrecorded shape the name guesses is not out-and-back); add-a-climb (`lib/AddRoute.jsx`) labels its box *Approach, one way* or, once *How you get back* is Loop / Point to point, *Total distance*, under the shared `HINTS.dist` (lib/fieldHints.js) that says one way vs whole outing; SuggestFix's box reads the same; a crag type (no distance box: SuggestFix `DROP` keeps `dist` off crags) has its Approach box ask for the distance and minutes by example; Compare's Approach row ends *one way* / *whole outing*; each way in's editor heads its distance / gain / hours row *Trailhead to the base, one way* and its card prints the distance *one way*, like the route's own figure beside it. `uDist` and `uDistMi` print one decimal in both units and two under 1 (metric used to print the raw number — "7.564 km" — and `uDistMi` rounded a 0.05 mi leg to "0.1 km"). The log form's three time legs each say where they start and end (*Trailhead to the base of the climb*, *Base of the climb to the top*, *Top back to the car*), since they sum to car-to-car.
- **A closed road is inside the distance** (owner note 2026-10-29). Where the road to a trailhead is washed out or closed, the stored distance and gain start where a climber can park, and `road.walkMi` (one way) / `road.walkGainFt` (the ascent the road adds) say how much of that is road. `TechStats` appends *Distance and ascent include about 8 mi each way on the closed road (…) and the 1,400 ft it climbs, from where you can park to the trailhead* (`roadWalkMi`, lib/outing.js). Both keys are in the road editor (`ROAD_KEYS`, lib/objKeys.js). Data is written per road after research (`scripts/oneoff/apply-closure-distance.mjs`, plans in `audits/closure-distance/`); candidates come from `npm run audit:closure-distance`.

A stored `gpx` that is only the route's own pins joined up (`lineIsBuiltFromPins`: its points sit on at least two of the route's pins, however many OTHER pins the route has — since 2026-10-28, when two camp pins added off a trailhead-to-summit chord had turned it into a "recorded track" on 21 routes) counts as no track: `dbRouteToCamel` drops it with its elevation profile (`recordedTrackPts`), `RouteDetail` drops it again after the approach overlay (`withRecordedTrackOnly`, `lib/track.js`), and `buildGpx` never exports it, so the map shows the pins alone, the button reads *Download GPX (waypoints only)*, and the *Add a track* prompt shows. A recorded track must also REACH THE SUMMIT (owner, 2026-10-28: "only use gpx tracks where the person reached the summit"): `reachesTheSummit` requires a track vertex within `SUMMIT_REACH_M` (300 m) of a summit pin, and a track that stops short counts as no track, with no caveat about it. A route with no summit pin cannot be checked, so its track is kept. A track must also have at least `MIN_RECORDED_PTS` (10) points (2026-10-29): 74 drawn lines had 2-9 points about 1.4 km apart, which no GPS records, so they count as no track like any other non-recording. ## 2. How it works

1. **Open.** `openRoute(x)` in `ClimbMatch.jsx` sets `routeFrom`, `selRoute` and `tab="routes"`. `App` renders `RouteDetail` (a lazy screen).
2. **Load (DB path, `USE_DB`).** `useAreaRoutes` (lib/db.js) backs the area browser's route list from `areas`/`routes`; `fetchRouteRow` fetches a single full row. `dbRouteToCamel()` converts a snake_case row to the camelCase shape the page reads. Seed `ROUTES` are only the fallback/demo set.
3. **Overlay of climber input.** `App` merges contributions onto the route inside its route memo: `useRouteContributions` supplies the route's contribution rows; only `status='approved'` rows are applied (value = `applied_value` else `value`), producing `_contribFields` (approved fields), `_pendingEdits` (the caller's OWN pending suggestions, shown as "Awaiting review") and `_contribAgreed` (how many climbers submitted the approved value; drives "✓ N climbers, checked" in the edit form). `useRouteDifficultyRatings` feeds the difficulty breakdown.
4. **Stars.** `useMyRouteRating(routeId, userId)` reads your `route_ratings` row into `myStars` (a failed read never clears the stars). `setRouteRating(userId, routeId, stars)` upserts, or deletes when `stars` is null, then invalidates `route-rating-mine` and `route-star-summary`. `useRouteStarSummary(routeId)` calls RPC `route_star_summary` for the shared average and rater count shown in the ★ cell.
5. **Sub-tabs** are rendered by `RouteDetail.jsx` off its `tab` state. The approach picker (`ApproachPicker`, lib/approaches.js) is the first card UNDER the sub-tab bar, on every tab, because the pick drives every tab. Its card is headed *HOW YOU GET THERE* with a blue rail; with more than one way in a compact radio chooser of the ways in sits at its top, and the card shows ONLY the picked way in's trailhead and link (no intro sentence, no note about Plan times). Each switchable variant may carry its own `hazards` and `objHaz`, which `applyApproach` (`overlayHazards`) lays over the row, so KNOWN HAZARDS on Safety follows the pick; a variant with no list keeps the row's. Audited by `check:approach-overlay`. *Other finishes from this route* (routes reached BY this one) is a separate card, on Overview only. A **variation** — a climb that leaves its via route partway and rejoins it, like Boving Roofs on the Southwest Rib — records the via route's pitches it also climbs as `viaPitches` (`{before:[1,3], after:[6,7]}`) on that way in: Overview then lists **The whole climb · N pitches** in climbing order (`wholeLine`), the via route's pitches read live from its own row; the Plan tab's time note names those pitches; the variation keeps its OWN trip plan instead of the via route's; and the parent's card reads *Variations of this route*, each chip saying where it leaves and rejoins (`viaPitchSpans`). Overview carries the consensus (`buildConsensus` over `route.activity`), hazard flags, route breakdown, approach/descent, social chips (`useRoutePairings`, `useRouteLoggedWith`). `FireNearRoute` is the first section of Safety on every route. A start of climb is filed from the *Start of climb* section of Add or fix info (or the log form) and shown as START OF CLIMB on Overview; Safety has no start-location button (removed 2026-10-15 as a duplicate). On a crag route (`cragOnly`) the top card is ROUTE FACTS — the number tiles, composite grade, aliases and first ascents — and the guidebook facts are three sections further down, built by `TechStats` with a `part` and drawn by `CragFactSection` (`CRAG_FACT_SECTIONS`): **LOCATION** (location, start) above GETTING THERE, then **GEAR & SAFETY** (bolts, protection, anchor, rope, fixed gear, landing, pads, descent) and **CONDITIONS** (aspect, sun, season, wet, rock quality) right under the RACK card. Facts on file come first; each missing one reads *Not on file yet* with its own **+ Add**, which opens the editor at that field (`FACT_FIELD`).
6. **Planner.** Time estimates come from `scarfHrs(...)` (Naismith-style approach by fitness tier and pack weight) and `techHrs(...)` (pitch-by-pitch climbing, exponential slowdown by grade); `lib/planTimes.js` (`planTimes`, `realPitches`) assembles the stage totals. A route's stored legs win over computed ones (`check:return-leg`). Each day of the trip plan shows its gain, loss, hours, miles and pack weight as ONE row of pills that share its width, so the pack weight no longer wraps onto a line of its own on a phone.
   **CAMPING & BIVY** (`CampingPanel`, under the calculator) merges `route.bivy` with Campsite pins (`campSites`): a pin and a site are one camp on an exact name, or under `samePlaceCamp` (both carry a height within 150 ft and the shorter name's distinctive words are all in the longer; upper/lower/middle count), and the pin lends its trail distance. A camp wears *Marked on the track* only when its pin has a coordinate the map draws (2026-10-28: 48 coordinate-less pins wore it); `npm run audit:camp-pins` checks every listed camp for a pin, a coordinate and a place on the recorded track. The sites are ONE list **in the order the walk in reaches them** (`campRouteOrder`, applied inside `campSites`, so the log form's *Where did you camp?* reads the same order): by trail distance from the trailhead, a site with no distance slotted in by its height, a site with neither last in stored order; the intro says so (`campOrderNote`). What a site is for is a tag on its card (`campRoleLabel`: *Where most parties sleep* / *Other option*, none when unsorted), not a group heading, and a card whose distance and the one above's are both known says *N mi on from <previous camp> · N ft higher* (`campFromPrev`). To help a party choose, each card can carry a green *Night N of the D-day trip plan* tag (`campNightsOf` reads only where an itinerary day ENDS: after the title's last "to", an arrival objective, or "return to") and *≈N h walk in at your planner pace* (`campWalkHrs`: `scarfHrs` over the camp's distance and gain at the fitness and pack set in the calculator, null without both). Each card also says *Slept here in N trip reports, latest <date>* from the log form's *Where did you camp?* (`campReportsOf` over the merged `activity`, matching a report's `cond.camps` to a site by NAME, case aside, nothing fuzzier; private reports count anonymously through `route_private_conditions`), and camps that reports name but the list lacks render under *NAMED IN TRIP REPORTS · NOT LISTED YET* with an *Add a camp* button that opens the same editor (nothing a report typed is written into the route). Guarded by `check:camping`. Its pencil (and *Contribute*) opens the **Camping & bivy** field of the edit sheet, filed under the group heading *Approach, descent & camping*: the row names the camps on file (`campNames`), every box in a site carries a visible caption, and a stored height is shown in the reader's units (`_bivyElevBox`) because the box is converted back to feet on save. Where `campingGate(route)` is false (a crag line that climbs no peak) the sheet drops the field and the heading reads *Approach & descent*, since no screen would show a camp filed there.
7. **Glacier terrain.** Alpine routes whose terrain read says glacier get the glacier conditions card plus the rock-weather flags (#2336).
8. **Photos.** `submitRoutePhoto` writes a `contributions` row of kind `photo`; `reportPhoto` reports one (into `content_reports`; there is no in-app queue since 2026-10-24 — read it in the Supabase dashboard); `lib/photoPrivacy.js` / `lib/photoEvidence.js` support gating.
9. **Suggest a fix on KNOWN HAZARDS (Safety).** `SafetyMatrix`'s header *✎ Edit* (the compact `EditIconButton` every route-page card header uses; its name is *Suggest a fix to the known hazards*) opens `SuggestFix` on its Hazards field with nothing picked; a *Fixed gear* chip opens it with that one hazard already picked (an extra chip beside the stock options). Hazards is a multi field, so the seed is always an ARRAY of strings — a bare string, or the click event the button once passed through, crashed the page.
   The BETA and PRO TIPS cards (Overview) use the same compact *✎ Edit* (named *Suggest a fix to the beta* / *…to the pro tips*); both open `SuggestFix` on Beta.
10. **Comments are live.** `useComments` (lib/db.js) is a `useLiveQuery` (`lib/live.js`): it subscribes over Supabase Realtime to `comments` and `comment_reactions`, and a row another climber inserts, edits, deletes or reacts to on any target this page holds (the route, its sub-tabs, pitches, photos, an area, a group post) refetches the comments, so the thread fills in without a reload (0326 put the tables in the `supabase_realtime` publication). The refetch goes through the same query and RLS as a reload.

## 4. Data

- **`routes`** (thousands of rows; `dbRouteToCamel` reads it). `routes.stars` is a cache kept by triggers on `route_ratings` and `climb_logs`; the Climbs list, min-stars filter and stars sort read it.
- **`route_ratings`** (0294): PK `(route_id, user_id)`, `stars` 1-5, RLS own-row read/insert/update/delete. Nothing renders who rated what.
- **`route_private_conditions(p_route)`** (0329, `useRoutePrivateConditions`): every report on the route the caller cannot read in full (kept to friends, a crew or the climber alone), as conditions only: date, condition/hazard tags, outcome and its reasons, times, snow/water/bugs/trail/protection/crowd answers, and an opaque per-route `reporter_key`. No author, text, photos, beta, partners or track; taken-down and blocked-either-way rows excluded. `anonRowToActivity` (`lib/tripReportRow.js`) marks them `_anon`; RouteDetail feeds them to the Overview consensus, `ConsensusPanel` and `AlpineConditionsCard` only (`condActivity`), never to a list of reports. This is Mountain Project's private-tick model: a private tick still counts on the route page without a name.
- **`route_views`** (0353): PK `(route_id, visitor, day)`, `visitor` an md5, RLS on with no policies — no client reads or writes a row. **`record_route_view(p_route, p_device)`** inserts one (ignored for an unknown route or a device key outside 16–64 characters); **`route_stats(p_route)`** returns one jsonb of counts: `views_total`, `views_30d`, `views_since`, `climbs`, `climbers`, `sends`, `senders`, `last_climbed`, `objectives`, `star_counts` (1★…5★), `sends_by_month` (Jan…Dec). Both callable signed out.
- **`route_star_summary(p_route_id)`**: average of one vote per climber: header rating, else their most recent public, visible trip report's stars; returns `stars` and `raters`.
- **`route_base_checkins`** (0188): one row per climber per route, written only through `check_in_at_route_base()`; others read unattributed SPOTS through `route_base_spots()` (0363; `useRouteBaseCheckins`, read once in `RouteDetail` and shared by the map and the panel; `route_base_checkin_points()` still returns raw points). A trigger feeds a confirmed spot into `routes.waypoints`' Base pin; **`route_base_pin_changes`** (owner-only, RLS with no policies) logs every placement, move and hold.
- **`contributions`**: corrections (held `pending` until the owner approves; 0307–0309) and route photos (kind `photo`). **`climb_logs`**: trip reports/stars.
- **Still seed/in-memory:** `ROUTES` fallback set and demo trip reports when not on the DB path.

## 5. Connections

- **Climbs browser:** `lib/DbAreaBrowser.jsx` and `openRoute` are the entry; the list's stars come from `routes.stars`.
- **Crews:** crew gear list reaches a real route (`check:crew-gear`); crews are built around a `routeId`.
- **Logbook:** logging a climb (`climb_logs`) feeds trip reports, consensus and, as fallback vote, the rating. Every report feeds the conditions: shared ones with their author, the rest anonymously through `route_private_conditions`.
- **Partners:** the `partners` sub-tab; `useRoutePairings` / `useRouteLoggedWith` show who logged it. On a crag route (0300) the sub-tab is "Climbers at <crag>":
  `usePartnersInArea` on the route's area, List me at <crag> (`onListMe`, into the My listed areas sheet), See all (`onFindPartnersInArea`), and the seed example cards are off;
  "Sessions at <crag>" lists open crews bound to exactly this route's area (`dbCrews` rows with an `areaId`), joinable like a route crew.
  On an alpine route it is still the climbers who saved it (`usePartnersByObjective`, match objectives only since 0303). Under that list, `savedNote` gives the whole count from `useRouteStats` (the same cached read as the ROUTE STATS card): "N climbers have this route on their objectives, you among them", and how many of them are not shown (unlisted, or blocked either way). It makes no gap claim when the RPC's 50-row page is full. A signed-out climber is asked to sign in instead of seeing the empty state.
- **Conditions:** see [conditions.md](conditions.md).

## 8. Known gaps / open decisions

- Live comments: Realtime applies RLS to an UPDATE, so a comment a moderator takes down is never SENT to other readers as a change. Since 0380 a trigger on `comments` broadcasts the bare id on the public `moderation` topic instead, and `lib/live.js` drops it from every screen that holds it at once (probe: `scripts/oneoff/probe-live-takedown.mjs`). A *restore* needs no broadcast: the row is readable again, so its UPDATE arrives normally.

- `SAR_BY_COUNTY` (`lib/sarByCounty.js`) holds one verified non-emergency dispatch line per county (579 counties, 2026-10-30). Each was read on the sheriff's, the county's or the county's dispatch centre's own page, or on a state agency's list of each county's 911 centre (Colorado PUC, Arkansas 911 Board, West Virginia EMD), labelled for that purpose; the page and the date for each are in `docs/codebase/rescue-dispatch-numbers.md`, never in the app. A county with no such page has no entry and shows 911 guidance only until a climber adds its line through the pencil. A route's stored `emergency.county` is read in any spelling ("Okanogan", "Okanogan County, WA", "Chelan County, Washington") through `countyPlace`. Emergency contacts are per ROUTE, so a climber's edit on one Castleton line does not reach its neighbours.

- A base check-in at a CRAG is judged against the crag's coordinate (500 m), so the server cannot tell which of two neighbouring lines a climber stood under; a spot is trustworthy only as far as several climbers agree on it. The find-the-base readout says so by handing over to the `location` note inside GPS error.

- #2403 states the corrections merge sits inside App's route memo and was not exercised against live contributions; only build guards cover it. It also records a database-path gap in `docs/guards/contributions-and-logbook.md`.
- #2336 did not run `check:ui` or `check:overflow` after the Total row grew from 3 to 4 tiles.
- Only 5 of 314,599 routes held a `stars` value when 0294 was written; a route's value is replaced only when a climber votes (per migration comment).
