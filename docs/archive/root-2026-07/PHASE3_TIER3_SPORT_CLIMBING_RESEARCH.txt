BASALT-SPECIFIC HAZARD DOCUMENTATION
================================================================================

COLUMNAR BASALT CHARACTERISTICS AFFECTING CLIMBING:

1. EXFOLIATION PATTERNS
- Natural sheeting occurs parallel to original lava surface
- Sheets 2-12 inches thick can detach unpredictably
- Freeze-thaw cycles accelerate separation (Oct-Apr most active)
- Climbing pressure can trigger exfoliation hours after initial contact
- Mitigation: Assume any hold could sheet off; use climbing movement that minimizes pressure

2. JOINTING GEOMETRY
- Vertical columnar joints create natural weakness planes
- Horizontal cooling fractures every 3-6 feet in height
- Creates hand/foot trap hazard (fingers wedge in joints)
- Can trap limbs or gear if climber falls into joint
- Mitigation: Size protection correctly; avoid jamming technique on unknown crags

3. WEATHERING PROFILE
- Upper sections (exposed to water/ice) weather faster (friable)
- Lower sections retain structural integrity longer
- Transition zone (middle height) has variable stability
- Rock quality testing required before committing to upper pitches
- Mitigation: Always inspect upper pitches before lead; test holds

4. VOLCANIC QUALITY VARIATION
- Original pillow structures influence strength distribution
- Vesicles (gas pockets) create hollow spots
- Glass content near surface creates smooth, unreliable texture on some walls
- Dark exterior oxidation can hide subsurface weakness
- Mitigation: Research specific area geology; approach unknown routes conservatively

5. BOLT CORROSION IN BASALT
- Basalt's porous structure retains moisture longer than granite
- Freeze-thaw cycles cause accelerated rust on steel bolts (esp. in direct rain zones)
- High desert environments have less direct rain but long-term corrosion still occurs
- Stainless steel hangers last significantly longer (where installed)
- Mitigation: Inspect all bolts visually; bring runner replacements
