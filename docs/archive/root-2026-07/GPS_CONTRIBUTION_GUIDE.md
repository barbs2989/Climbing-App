# 📱 How to Contribute GPS Data to ClimbMatch

Help complete the WA alpine routes database! If you've climbed one of our routes and have GPS data from your watch or device, we'd love your contribution.

---

## Which Routes Need GPS Data?

These 16 WA alpine routes still need GPS tracks from climbers:

- Mount Terror: Stoddard Buttress, West Ridge
- Guye Peak: South Gully/South Spur, North Route
- Little Sister: North Face, West Face
- Little Tahoma: Cowlitz/Ingraham Glaciers

- Dragontail Peak: Triple Couloirs

- Mount Maude: East Ridge

**Check your watch.** If you've climbed any of these routes, your GPS track is valuable!

## Step 1: Get Your GPS Data

### From a Smartwatch (Apple Watch, Garmin, Suunto)
1. Open the fitness app on your watch
2. Find the climbing activity from your ascent date
3. Export as GPX file or copy coordinates
4. Save to your phone

**How to export:**
- **Apple Watch + iPhone:** Open Health app → Activity → Segment → Share → Save to Files
- **Garmin:** Connect to Garmin Connect website → Activities → Export as GPX
- **Suunto:** Suunto app → Activity → Menu → Export → GPX

### From GPS Device (Handheld Garmin, etc.)
1. Connect device to computer via USB
2. Open file manager
3. Navigate to GARMIN/GPX folder
4. Find file from climb date
5. Transfer to phone or email

### From Phone (AllTrails, Strava, Komoot, Caltopo)
1. Open the app where you recorded the route
2. Find activity from climb date
3. Look for "Share" or "Export" button
4. Select GPX format
5. Copy coordinates or download file

### From Manual Recording
1. Open any GPS logger app on your phone (MyTracks, GPXLogger, etc.)
2. Look for saved tracks from climb date
3. Export as GPX or share coordinates

## Step 2: Prepare Your Data

### What Should Your Track Include?

**GOOD track:**
- ✓ Starts at trailhead parking area
- ✓ Follows the actual climbing route (not roads)
- ✓ Includes major waypoints (camps, crux sections)
- ✓ Ends at peak summit
- ✓ 5+ waypoints minimum
- ✓ Elevation gain matches expected grade
- ✓ Natural terrain-following GPS points (not straight lines)

**Example good track for Disappointment Cleaver (Rainier):**
```
Point 1: Paradise parking (5,400 ft)
Point 2: Muir Snowfield mid-point (8,000 ft)
Point 3: Camp Muir (10,080 ft)
Point 4: Ingraham Flats (11,400 ft)
Point 5: High Crack crevasse crossing (11,200 ft)
Point 6: Summit area (14,410 ft)
= Good! 6 points, clear progression, realistic elevation
```

**POOR track:**
- ✗ Starts at nearby town instead of trailhead
- ✗ Includes road drive data
- ✗ Only 2-3 waypoints
- ✗ Ends far from summit
- ✗ GPS dropout zones (blank spots)
- ✗ Unrealistic straight-line connections

## Step 3: Submit Your Track

### Option A: In-App Submission (Easiest)
1. Open ClimbMatch app
2. Find the route you climbed
3. Click the **📍 "Help improve this route"** banner
4. Click **"Submit GPS Track"**
5. Fill out the form:
   - **Route confirmation:** "Yes, I personally climbed this route"
   - **Climb date:** [select date]
   - **GPS source:** Apple Watch / Garmin / Strava / etc.
   - **Paste coordinates** or **upload .gpx file**
   - **Verify:** "This is the climbing route, not the approach"
   - **Optional note:** Any details about conditions, variations, etc.
6. Click **"Submit"**

### Option B: Email Submission
If in-app doesn't work for you:

Email to: **routes@climbmatch.app**

Subject: `GPS Contribution: [Route Name]`

Include:
- Your name (or "Anonymous")
- Climb date
- Route name and peak
- GPX file attachment or pasted coordinates
- Brief note about track quality/conditions

Example:
```
Subject: GPS Contribution: Stoddard Buttress, Mount Terror

Hi,
I climbed Stoddard Buttress on Mount Terror on July 20, 2026.
Attached is my GPS track from my Garmin watch.
The route was in good condition, clear trail.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

## Step 4: What Happens Next?

1. **We receive your submission** — Thanks for contributing! 🎉
2. **We validate the data** — Check that your track meets quality standards (5+ points, realistic elevation, etc.)
3. **We reach out if questions** — We may email to clarify route details
4. **We merge your GPS** — Your track gets added to the route in ClimbMatch
5. **You get credited** — Your name appears on the route as contributor
6. **Climbers benefit** — Next time someone plans this climb, they see your GPS data

## Quality Standards

### We'll accept your track if:
- ✅ You personally climbed the route (not someone else's data)
- ✅ Track has 5+ waypoints
- ✅ Route starts at known trailhead
- ✅ Route ends at peak summit (within 100 ft)
- ✅ Elevation gain/loss makes sense for the grade
- ✅ GPS points follow terrain (not straight lines or teleports)
- ✅ You confirm it's the climbing route, not approach variations

### We'll ask questions if:
- ⚠️ Track is short (less than 5 points)
- ⚠️ Elevation seems off
- ⚠️ Route ends far from summit
- ⚠️ GPS quality looks degraded
- ⚠️ Multiple route variations possible

### We won't accept if:
- ❌ You haven't climbed the route
- ❌ It's someone else's data (ask them to submit!)
- ❌ Track quality is too poor to be useful
- ❌ Coordinates are way off expected location

## Troubleshooting

### "My watch didn't record GPS"
- Apple Watch needs iPhone nearby for GPS
- Smartwatches need to have GPS enabled before the climb
- Some watches use phone's GPS instead of onboard chip
- If no GPS: manual waypoint markers help (if you have them)

### "I lost my GPS file"
- Check cloud backups (iCloud, Google Fit, Garmin Connect, Strava)
- Check fitness app history (some apps store backup copies)
- If you remember major waypoints, you can manually enter coordinates

### "My track has GPS dropouts"
- That's OK! Submit anyway with a note about the gaps
- We can interpolate via topo map if route is well-known
- Partial data is better than none

### "I'm not sure which route variation I climbed"
- No problem! Leave a note describing your route
- Tell us which camps/crux sections you passed
- We can figure out which route it was

### "The route is private/secret — I don't want to share GPS"
- Totally understandable
- Check first if the route is already publicly documented
- If it's a real climbing route in a guidebook, sharing helps everyone
- If it's a true secret spot, respect that call
