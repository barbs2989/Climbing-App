# Full-app bug audit — 2026-07-30

Audited at `origin/main` e6e6106 (PRs through #464). Method: ten scoped review passes
(Today, Discover, Climbs browser, route-detail info tabs, planner/safety, crew
lifecycle, messaging/groups, Logbook/Ranks, Me/profile, contribute/auth/shell, plus a
computational-core pass), each tracing every claim to a concrete triggering input.
PRs #457–#464 merged mid-audit, so all high-severity findings from the two earliest
passes were re-verified against e6e6106; line numbers below are current at that commit.
Live-DB claims (string coords, waypoint fallbacks, obj_haz counts) were checked against
the production Supabase data, not seed.

Severity: **H** = crash, data loss/corruption, or a primary flow broken in production
config (USE_DB). **M** = wrong data shown, dead feature, or dishonest copy. **L** = minor,
latent, or demo-only.

Counts: ~130 distinct findings — 41 H, 55 M, ~34 L. Recurring root causes:

1. **Seed-shaped assumptions on the DB path** — `routeById`/`MOUNTAINS.find`/`CLIMBERS.find`
   resolve only seed data; uuid vs `id 0` identity mismatches. (D2, D3, R8, W7, M5, L17, E-series.)
2. **Write-path field maps drifting from read paths** — snake/camel and name mismatches
   silently drop fields. (L2 `fa`/`fa_ascent`, L3 tickType, L5 partners, X1 whitelist, comment authorName.)
3. **RLS-rejected writes swallowed and presented as saved.** (W1, W3, W9, W11, L11.)
4. **Copy asserting mechanisms that do not exist.** (E1 catch confirmation, W8/W16, M6 "Seen",
   M8/M9, P7 offline pack, X10 provenance confirm.)
5. **Fabricated/hash-derived data presented as real.** (A1 availability, D7 mutual friends,
   E6 "government ID", A10 years climbing.)

**A1 is narrower and sharper than written.** The finding says availability is fabricated
for "all but one seed climber + all fillers" — true, and those are all fictional demo
people, so the hash itself is roughly as dishonest as their fictional grades. The part
that matters for real users is the other half: a hydrated profile gets
`availability: []`, and `availMatch` returns **true** when either side is empty. So a
real climber who never entered a schedule passes every AVAILABLE filter, sorts to the
top of a date search as if matched, and renders a green **"Likely free your date"** badge
— plus a dangling `"Usually free: "` with nothing after the colon. Removing the hash
without teaching the call sites that empty means *unknown* would widen that false claim
rather than fix it.

### Two traps this pass hit

- **A short symbol collides across the split files.** X1's merge whitelist is
  `var SS={grade:1,name:1,…}` on the `ClimbMatch.jsx` mega-line, next to `CONV` and `M`.
  `RouteDetail.jsx` has an unrelated `const SS=["N","NNE",…]` holding compass directions.
  Grepping `SS` in the contribute UI finds the compass array and reads as a hit.
- **Contribution merging is not `enrichRoute`.** `enrichRoute` merges *static enrichment*
  data. The contribute path is `CONV` (convert) → `SS` (whitelist) → `M` (rename), all on
  that same mega-line.

### The two that were live

**W8 — the float plan told climbers something untrue about their own safety.** Three
defects in one handler. The toast said *"your contact is notified if you don't check
in"*: nothing schedules a check-in, nothing reads `float_plan` server-side, and no
notification path exists. That is the most dangerous kind of false claim in this app,
because a climber who believes it may skip telling an actual person. It also wrote
through `setCrews`, so the plan never reached the database even though `updateCrew`
already maps `floatPlan → float_plan` — a safety record that disappears on refresh.
And when the emergency contact or trip date were unknown it stored the literal strings
`"your emergency contact"` and `"trip day"` as the data. Now: persisted through
`updateCrew`, unknown fields stored as `null`, and the copy says plainly that you must
send the plan yourself. Note `floatPlan.contact` / `.returnBy` / `.filedAt` are currently
written but never rendered — only the plan's existence is shown.

**E1 — the belay record claimed a two-party confirmation that does not exist.** Four
strings asserted it, including *"Catches are confirmed by both climbers, so a belay
record can't be faked"* and an FAQ answer promising a *"verified catch ledger"*. The
`belay_catches` table has no confirmation column at all — `logBelajCatch` inserts
`belayer_id, climber_id, date_occurred, description` and there is no confirm mutation, so
a catch counts on one party's word. This one is load-bearing: partners use the catch
record to decide who to rope up with. The copy now describes what actually happens — a
logged catch names the other climber and appears on both records, nothing asks them to
confirm it, so read it as a claim rather than proof — and points at vouches, which do come
from the other climber, as the stronger signal. Building real confirmation is a feature,
and is deliberately not attempted here.
