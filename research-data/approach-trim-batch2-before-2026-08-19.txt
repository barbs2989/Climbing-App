wa_blackcap_mountain_scramble — Standard Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. East face to the northeast ridge
  2. Northeast ridge to the summit

--- approach, in full ---
Blackcap sits deep in the Pasayten Wilderness and is reached almost entirely off-trail after a long, multi-day trail approach — there is no short car-to-summit option. The standard approach starts at the Robinson Creek/Buckskin Ridge Trailhead (~6,900 ft) at the end of Slate Peak Road (FS-5400, the spur above Harts Pass), the highest trailhead in Washington reached by road. From the trailhead, follow the ridge/trail over Slate Pass and drop down into the West Fork Pasayten drainage, picking up Robinson Creek/Middle Fork Pasayten Trail #478 (the full trail runs ~23 miles one-way through this country). Continue down-valley past Robinson Pass to the Middle Fork Pasayten River (roughly 9 miles in, then a further ~3-mile descent to the river) — most parties camp here or push on the first day (about 5 hours trailhead-to-camp at a backpacking pace). Day two, follow the Middle Fork Pasayten downstream, then turn up past Fred's Lake (a reliable water source and common camp) and climb to 7,100-ft Rolo Pass on the ridge crest above the lake; Lake Doris is a short side trip from here. From Rolo Pass, drop into Upper Eureka Creek (another dependable water source) and cross east into scenic Shellrock Basin, then climb to 7,500-ft Shellrock Pass — a full trail day of roughly 2.5-3 hours from Fred's Lake. Camp is typically made in Shellrock Basin, just below and east of the pass. From camp, leave the trail at Shellrock Pass and traverse SSW across an open, grassy/rocky basin, staying below the cliff bands, to the base of a talus slope; work the left (east) edge of the talus south to a saddle/col just west of the cliff terminus, directly below Blackcap's east face — this off-trail link is about 1-1.5 miles and 1,000-1,500 ft of gain/loss with real route-finding (a steep snowfield below Shellrock Pass often needs to be bypassed on the left in early season — carry an axe if snow persists). From the col, scramble up loose scree and talus on Blackcap's east face to gain the northeast ridge, where the rock transitions to distinctive black hornfels/shale; follow the ridge (staying on its left/south side as terrain allows) on easy Class 2-3 with some exposure and loose rock near the top to the summit. Camp-to-summit is about 5-6 hours round trip. An alternate eastern approach exists via the Monument Creek Trail, leaving it a few miles before Butte Pass where Monument Creek bends west into the open Monument-Blackcap basin, then climbing that basin's cliff band on either flank to reach the same upper mountain — but the Shellrock Pass approach described above is the standard, most-traveled line. Either way this is a serious wilderness undertaking: expect 30-35+ round-trip miles and 3-5 days car-to-car, with the Middle Fork Pasayten River crossing running high and cold in early season and long stretches of trail that can be brushy or storm-damaged in a given year. The ridge includes one true knife-edge section with easy-to-moderate exposure, distinct from the otherwise mellow class 2-3 terrain.

wa_hurry_up_peak_south_ridge — South Ridge  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Steep snow band on the south/southwest flank
  2. Broken granite slabs to the south ridge crest
  3. South ridge crest and upper gully system

--- approach, in full ---
Start at the Cascade Pass Trailhead (~3,600 ft) at the end of Cascade River Road (about 23 miles of pavement then gravel from Marblemount; the upper gravel section is gated and typically doesn't open until snow clears, often not until late June/July). From the trailhead, follow the well-graded, heavily-used Cascade Pass Trail 3.7 miles and about 1,800 ft of gain, switchbacking up through forest and avalanche-path meadows to Cascade Pass (5,392 ft) — the last reliable trailside water is at the creek crossings low on the trail, so top off bottles before the switchbacks. At the pass, leave the maintained tread and pick up the climbers' path/boot track heading south-southeast, making a rising traverse across snow or heather on the flanks of Mixup Peak to the base of the Cache Glacier (~6,000 ft); this stretch is loose, choosy, and crosses several snow patches that can be firm in early morning. Ascend moderate glacier/snow slopes (crevasses can open by late summer — rope up if the party isn't comfortable soloing them) to the notch at Cache Col (6,900 ft). From the col, descend and traverse south-southeast about 1 mile over loose rock and heather benches, crossing occasional snowfields, down to Kool-Aid Lake (6,320 ft), the standard base camp for this side of the Ptarmigan Traverse and the only dependable water source in the basin (may be partly frozen early season). Car-to-camp is roughly 5-6 miles with about 3,300 ft of gain, generally a half-day carrying overnight gear. From camp, the peak itself is climbed as a side trip: head up toward Hurry-up Peak's south/southwest flank, crossing a band of steep snow around 7,100 ft (carry an axe/crampons even mid-summer) before gaining broken granite slabs and the south ridge crest; follow the crest/upper gully system with sustained Class 2-3 scrambling and a couple of exposed but short moves along the summit ridge to the 7,821 ft top — about 1 mile and 1,400-1,500 ft above camp, roughly 1-1.5 hours for a fit party. Route-finding on the granite benches is the main crux; parties report multiple viable gully lines, not all matching guidebook description exactly.

wa_lake_mountain_pasayten_scramble — Standard Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. East ridge to the summit
  2. Northwest ridge variation

--- approach, in full ---
Lake Mountain (8,377') sits in a remote basin of the eastern Pasayten Wilderness immediately southeast of Monument Peak, and there is no short way in — both viable approaches are 2-3 day backpack-and-scramble trips. Primary approach: Monument Creek Trail No. 484, which leaves Harts Pass Road about 6 miles northwest of Mazama at the Monument Creek/Lost River trailhead (~2,400'). The trail follows the Lost River valley north for about 4 miles to Eureka Creek, where the old trail bridge has been out since it washed out (reports place this as early as 2006) — the ford is unbridged and can run thigh-to-waist deep during snowmelt season (June-early July); treat it as a real hazard, not a formality, and time the crossing for lower morning flows if possible. Beyond Eureka Creek the tread becomes an abandoned, unmaintained trail climbing onto the crest of Pistol Ridge — expect heavy deadfall, brush, and stretches where the path disappears entirely and is followed by cairns alone. This stretch gains roughly 5,000' over about 7-8 miles to 7,100' Pistol Pass. There is no reliable water between the Eureka Creek ford (mile ~4) and the Lake of the Woods basin, so tank up at the crossing. From Pistol Pass, descend about a mile north past Lake of the Woods to a basecamp around 7,000' — total is roughly 12 miles and 5,300' of gain from trailhead to camp. Alternate approach: from 6,960' Slate Pass at the end of Harts Pass Road, descend to the Robinson Creek (Middle Fork Pasayten) Trail No. 478 and follow it downstream about 7 miles to a large meadow/horse camp, then pick up the unmarked, unmaintained Ferguson Lake Trail No. 474A, which climbs an open avalanche chute and sidehills to Ferguson Lake in a cirque below Wildcat Mountain before dropping into the Eureka Creek drainage just south of Monument Peak — this avoids the Eureka Creek ford but is longer and its own tread is harder to follow. From either camp, gain the low pass just south of Lake Mountain, drop down the far side, and make a descending traverse into the basin between Monument Peak and Lake Mountain. From here a subsidiary rock knob (Pk. 8165) blocks a direct line up the south side, so the route bends onto the mountain's east ridge — a sustained but pleasant scramble on exposed, blocky Class 2-3 granite — to the summit; this final push from Lake of the Woods camp runs about 2-4 miles round trip with 1,300-1,400' of gain and takes under 2 hours each way for a fit party. An older, less-used line to the summit follows the northwest ridge from Pistol Pass before cutting southeast, as an alternative to the west-slope/east-ridge line from the Lake of the Woods camp described above.

wa_luahna_peak_east_slopes — East Slopes  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Benches and ledges to the col west of Clark Mountain
  2. Upper Richardson Glacier traverse
  3. East Ridge to the summit
  4. Variation: south/southeast ridge (avoids the glacier)

--- approach, in full ---
From the White River Trailhead (~2,300 ft, at the end of White River Road/FR 6400, reached via WA-207 and White River Road from Coles Corner off US-2), follow the White River Trail (#1507) upstream along a mostly level grade through old-growth forest for about 4 miles to its junction with the Boulder Pass Trail (#1562, also called the Boulder Creek Trail). Water is abundant the whole way — the White River parallels the trail, and Boulder Creek is a dependable source at the junction (the crossing here can be a ford depending on the year/season, so check recent trip reports before committing). Turn onto the Boulder Pass Trail and climb switchbacks through forest, gaining roughly 2,500-2,700 ft over about 3 miles to break out into the open basin below Boulder Pass (~5,000 ft). Because the round trip from the trailhead runs in the 20+ mile range with well over 6,000 ft of gain, most parties do this as an overnight, camping in the Boulder Pass basin or pushing on to a high camp in Upper Boulder Basin (~5,800 ft). From the basin, leave the maintained trail for the unmaintained 'sheepherder's path,' a boot track that sidehills east across a series of cirques (Clark Basin, then Luahna Basin), losing roughly 1,000 ft as it traverses below Clark Mountain's south spur before regaining elevation near a 7,200 ft spur. This traverse is the route-finding crux of the approach — brushy, exposed to sidehill slips, and easy to lose in poor visibility or snow cover. From the spur, a rising traverse across broad benches (often snow-covered well into summer) and easy Class 2 ledges leads to the ~7,600 ft col west of Clark Mountain; from there a rising traverse across the upper Richardson Glacier (real crevasse hazard — carry glacier travel gear, and note the glacier has been reported thinning/broken-up and less straightforward in recent years) reaches Luahna's East Ridge, which is scrambled west to the summit. Parties wanting to avoid the glacier crossing can instead stay on rock from the basin and scramble Luahna's south/southeast ridge directly (Class 2-3), which several sources describe as the more reliable line in low-snow years.

wa_mount_bigelow_scramble — Standard Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Slabby rock above the lower cliff band
  2. Traverse left below the upper cliff band
  3. Summit block finish
  4. Variation: west slopes from Horsehead Pass

--- approach, in full ---
Drive to the Crater Creek Trailhead (4,750 ft): from Twisp, take WA-20 east 2 miles to WA-153, then south 12 miles to County Rd 1029/1034 (signed for Foggy Dew Campground) and follow it about 6 miles as it becomes FR 4340 - pavement ends around mile 4 (bear right) - then continue on FR 4340-300 roughly 4.6 more miles to the trailhead parking area (vault toilet, stock ramp, no potable water). From the trailhead, hike the Eagle Lakes Trail #431 about 5-6 miles, gaining roughly 2,400 ft, to the basin holding Lower Eagle Lake (6,490 ft) and Upper Eagle Lake (7,110 ft) - the trail passes an overlook of Lower Eagle Lake around mile 5.5 with a junction just beyond it (left to Lower Eagle Lake/Horsehead Pass, right ~0.5 mi to Upper Eagle Lake). For the direct line, leave the trail near Upper Eagle Lake and head northwest through larch stands toward a notch, staying right of the prominent rock wall above the lake; scramble slabby rock to get above a lower cliff band, then traverse left below the next cliff band and climb steep, loose scree/rock to just below the summit block for the Class 3 finish. For the easier line, continue past Lower Eagle Lake up to Horsehead Pass and climb the peak's west slopes.

wa_mount_fernow_southeast_face — Southeast Face / Leroy Basin  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. South/southeast talus and scree slopes
  2. Upper choss to the summit block

--- approach, in full ---
From the Phelps Creek Trailhead (3,500 ft, at the end of the Phelps Creek spur off Chiwawa River Road/FR-62), follow Phelps Creek Trail #1511 south. The tread is in generally good maintained shape, with only occasional overgrown brush in late summer. At about 3.5 miles (roughly 1.5-2 hours in), the trail runs alongside Leroy Creek — a large waterfall visible up-valley from the switchbacks is a good landmark — where an unsigned but well-beaten climbers' path breaks off on the north (true right) side of the creek. This use trail is unmaintained but easy to follow from steady foot traffic; it climbs very steeply, gaining roughly 2,000 ft in about 1.5-1.9 miles while staying above Leroy Creek, which is the last reliable water before the basin. It eases into Leroy Creek Basin (~6,050-6,100 ft, about 5.4 mi and ~2,700 ft gain from the trailhead), a broad heather-and-boulder cirque that is the standard base camp, with good tent sites and running water. From camp most parties continue north/northeast across meadow and rock toward a col (~7,800 ft) on Seven Fingered Jack's south shoulder, roughly another hour. This whole upper section is cross-country with sparse cairns — Fernow itself isn't visible from most of Leroy Basin, and the peak has a well-earned reputation for tricky route-finding, so carry a map/GPS track rather than counting on a visible target. From the col, the route loses about 1,000 ft into the head of the Ice/Big Creek drainage, crossing beneath a shrinking glacier remnant (open crevasses have been reported here — rope up for the crossing or give it a wide berth), then curves west onto the broad talus/scree slopes on Fernow's south/southeast side and climbs roughly a mile toward the summit block, mostly class 2-3 talus with one notably exposed 'unexpected crux' — a sloped, gravel-covered slab with about a 30 m drop below and a 10 m traverse across it, rated only class 2 but consequential. Above about 8,800 ft the terrain steepens into class 3-4 choss for the final several hundred feet to the base of the summit block.

wa_mount_pershing_standard — Standard Scramble (Jefferson Creek / Hamma Hamma approach)  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Rocky gully to the 4,300 ft basin
  2. Final snow/rock gully to the ridge
  3. Ridge crest to the true (south) summit

--- approach, in full ---
From Hoodsport, drive about 14 miles north on US 101 to Hamma Hamma River Road (FR 25); follow it roughly 6.5 miles to FR 2480, then FR 2480 about 3 miles to FR 2401, then FR 2401 roughly 7–7.5 miles to the Jefferson Creek crossing / Jefferson Lake Trailhead area (~2,440 ft). A faint boot path on the north side of the creek leads through forest and brush into the basins below Pershing's east side, then up gullies to the summit ridge. Confirm current road status with Olympic National Forest before driving — FR 2480/2401 have a history of washouts and were briefly closed in the 2025 Hamma Fire (closure since rescinded). From the boot path, the route rises via a rocky gully to a small basin around 4,300 ft, then continues into a basin east of the summit near 5,400 ft before the final snow/rock gully to the ridge. The ridge crest to the true (south) summit is mostly Class 2 with occasional Class 3 and an exposed knife-edge section reported by some parties.

wa_sky_mountain_s_route — South Slopes Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. East ridge of Skyline/Heather Ridge
  2. False sub-summit to the true summit

--- approach, in full ---
From the Stevens Pass Ski Area's north (uphill) lot, walk to the far east end of the parking area near the small gray electrical substation/WSDOT building — this is the unsigned Skyline Lake trailhead. In summer, take the gravel service road on the left side of the substation, passing beneath the powerlines; a steeper, faster boot path also splits off directly across from the WSDOT building for hikers who want to skip the road switchbacks. Follow the road/track uphill, staying left, then bear right shortly after — a vehicle gate blocks the road about a quarter mile up. The road/trail climbs steadily (roughly 500 ft of gain per half-mile in the steepest stretch) through forest and talus for about 1.2-1.3 miles to Skyline Lake (~1,100-1,300 ft gained, lake sits around 4,900-5,100 ft). Pass Skyline Lake on its south side. Just before the ground steepens into the first rocky scrambling, watch for a large boulder garden — car- to house-sized blocks — and angle southwest through/around it to regain forest on the far side; this talus-hopping section is the main route-finding wrinkle on the whole approach and is slow and ankle-twisty when wet or snow-covered. Beyond the boulder garden, gain the undulating east ridge of the horseshoe-shaped Skyline/Heather Ridge and follow it west, traversing beneath a band of rocky outcrops before the final push to Sky Mountain's summit block. Total one-way distance to the true summit is about 2.5 miles with roughly 1,400-2,600 ft of cumulative gain depending on how much of the undulating ridge is counted (the route re-gains elevation repeatedly along the ridge, which is why on-file gain (2,600 ft) runs higher than the simple lake-to-summit elevation difference). There is no reliable water past Skyline Lake, so treat/carry water from there. The exposed south-facing slopes below the ridge are a known avalanche path — several fatal slides have occurred here in winter — so this route is best done in summer/early autumn when snow-free; a faint climbers' path is reported to persist most of the way to the summit in those conditions. The undulating ridge tops out at a lower, rockier sub-summit that most parties mistake for the true summit; reaching the actual high point requires a steep traverse on the south side of that outcrop before a short final climb.
