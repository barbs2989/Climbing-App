wa_buck_mountain_south_ridge — South Ridge  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Pumice and talus slopes to the south ridge
  2. South ridge crest to the summit

--- approach, in full ---
From the Trinity Trailhead (Glacier Peak Wilderness, self-issue wilderness permit at the kiosk) at the end of the Chiwawa River Road/FR-6200 (~2,800 ft), follow Buck Creek Trail #1513. The trail splits from the Chiwawa River Trail #1550 within the first mile and climbs steadily through forest; around mile 2.75-3 it crosses the upper Chiwawa River on a footbridge that is weathered/damaged, so treat it with care in high runoff and have a wading backup plan in early season. Above the crossing the trail is a long, fairly shadeless, grinding ascent (mosquitoes/black flies are heavy in July) through forest and then subalpine meadow along the flank of Middle Ridge, opening into increasingly good wildflower and Glacier Peak views as you climb. At roughly 9.25-10 miles and about 3,000-3,300 ft of gain, the trail tops out at Buck Creek Pass (~5,800-5,900 ft) - one of the most scenic passes in the Cascades, with a reliable spring near the pass for water (last dependable source before the basin). Beyond the pass there is no maintained trail: pick up the well-used climbers'/boot path that switchbacks up toward Liberty Cap and the High Pass Trail junction, then leave that path to contour and traverse west/southwest, staying below Liberty Cap, into the upper basin below Buck Mountain's south and west aspects (Louis Creek Basin, ~6,700 ft) - the standard camp for a two-day trip. From the basin, ascend broad pumice and talus slopes and a low-angle snowfield (an ice axe, and crampons in early season, are worth carrying) to gain the south ridge around 7,400 ft. Follow the ridge crest through Class 2-3 terrain (stepping to locally 3-4 near a horn/gendarme close to the true summit) on loose, rubbly, slabby schist to the top; the summit crest has several bumps within feet of each other, so take care identifying the true high point. Primary approach hazards: rockfall/loose schist (helmet recommended, especially with parties above or below), the river crossing in high water, no reliable water source between the pass and the basin in a dry year, and route-finding above Buck Creek Pass since the climbers' path is intermittent. An alternate historic approach reaches Buck Mountain via Little Giant Pass and the Napeequa Valley, used for longer traverses.

wa_chimney_rock_west_face — West Face / South Summit (Standard)  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Lower Chimney Glacier to the toe of the south peak
  2. Pitch 1 — grooves and cracks right of the Rappel Chimney

--- approach, in full ---
This route shares the same trailhead-to-basin approach as the East Face: from the Pete Lake Trailhead off FR 4616, hike the Pete Lake Trail #1323 (~4 easy miles to Pete Lake, then ~2 more miles) to the Pacific Crest Trail junction, head north on the PCT about a mile to the Chimney Creek avalanche-path crossing, then leave the PCT and follow the climber's path (flagged/cairned, fords Chimney Creek and crosses several brushy ravines) up through Sunrise Knob (~4,650 ft, ~4-5 hrs from the trailhead) to the moraine and the bivy basin at the toe of the Chimney Glacier (~5,400 ft) — see the East Face route's approach text for full turn-by-turn detail on this shared stretch, and treat it as a 2-3 day car-to-car trip. From the bivy basin, rope up and cross the lower Chimney Glacier, working toward the toe of the south peak rather than the main-summit gully used by the East Face. Route-description sources place the start of Pitch 1 in grooves and cracks roughly 30-40 feet climber's-right of the obvious 'Rappel Chimney' feature on the west face, reached via a short glacier crossing and scramble from the basin; published beta on this specific glacier-to-base connection is thinner than for the East Face line, so budget extra route-finding time and be ready to improvise around the moat at the base of the face, which gets worse later in the season. Water and camping options match the East Face approach: Pete Lake, the low Chimney Creek crossings, or melted glacier snow above the moraine.

wa_colfax_peak_cosley_houston — Cosley-Houston Couloir  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Bergschrund to the base of the couloir

--- approach, in full ---
From the Heliotrope Ridge trailhead (~3,700 ft, end of Glacier Creek Road/FR 39), follow the Heliotrope Ridge Trail (#677) through forest for roughly 2 miles, crossing a plank bridge over Grouse Creek and one avalanche-path/rockfall gully below Hogsback that can hold hazardous debris and running water in warm conditions. The trail breaks out of trees near a viewpoint of Coleman Glacier icefall around 4,900-5,000 ft; this is the last reliable running water before the moraine. From there, gain the lateral moraine and the well-worn Coleman-Deming boot track (the standard Mount Baker route), staying on or near the crest of the moraine before dropping onto glacier ice. Rope up for glacier travel here — crevasse hazard is real and grows through the season. Contour/ascend the Coleman Glacier heading climber's right (northeast) toward the prominent bench/rognon beneath the north faces of Colfax Peak, staying low and left around a well-known large crevasse that guards the direct line; parties commonly reach this bench in 3 to 4.5 hours from the car with good booting conditions, slower after new snow or when the skin track has been chewed up by skiers. From the bench, cross or tunnel through the bergschrund (often the route's crux obstacle — overhanging and requiring a mixed traverse from the right in leaner years) to gain the base of the couloir/gully system just right of the Polish Route. Total gain to the route base near 8,000 ft is on the order of 4,500-4,700 ft over roughly 4-5 miles one-way; the upper glacier and bergschrund crossing should be done early before the north-facing slopes see any sun-warming, and this route/aspect avalanches after new snow, so avoid climbing after significant snowfall or in elevated avalanche danger.

wa_mount_ballard_south — South Slopes  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. South slopes and gullies from Azurite Pass

--- approach, in full ---
Standard approach: drive Highway 20 to the Canyon Creek Trailhead near Mazama and follow the Mill Creek/Azurite Pass Trail (#755) up Canyon Creek then Mill Creek — about 5 miles to the site of the defunct Azurite Mine, then a further ~3 increasingly faint, brush-choked miles (slide alder and downed timber are common since the trail has seen little maintenance since the mine closed) to roughly the 14-mile mark, where a boot path continues about 1.5 more miles to Azurite Pass (6,680+ ft). From the pass, leave the trail and scramble northeast/east up Ballard's broad south-facing slopes and gullies, watching for the correct gully to the true summit block. A longer alternate, more often used for combined Azurite-Ballard trips, starts at the West Fork Methow River Trailhead (#480) near River Bend Campground: follow that trail ~8 miles to the PCT, then the PCT south ~1.5 miles to Horse Heaven Camp before joining the Mill Creek Trail to the pass — this option runs closer to 25 miles round trip with about 6,000 ft of elevation gain.

wa_mount_carru_scramble — South Slopes Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. South/southeast talus slopes

--- approach, in full ---
Two standard approaches leave from the Harts Pass Road (FR 5400) area near Slate Peak (~6,900-7,400 ft, one of the highest drivable roads in Washington): (1) the shortest line drops from Slate Pass/Buckskin Ridge down the West/Middle Fork Pasayten River to Fred's Lake/Fred's Pass, then crosses upper Eureka Creek toward Shellrock Basin below the Carru-Lago-Osceola cirque — about 15 miles one-way; (2) the Monument Creek Trail, also off Harts Pass Road, is roughly 19 miles one-way with more up-and-down elevation change. From a Shellrock Basin or Eureka Creek camp, gain the Carru-Osceola col or Rolo Pass and scramble the south/southeast talus slopes to the summit ridge. Pasayten trails are lightly maintained — expect blowdown on the Middle Fork Pasayten Trail and stretches where the tread is fainter than GPS basemaps suggest, so off-trail navigation skill is required.

wa_mount_tom_scramble — Glacier/Scramble Route  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Blue Glacier and Snow Dome
  2. Upper massif traverse to Mount Tom
  3. Summit block scramble

--- approach, in full ---
From the Hoh River Trailhead (end of Upper Hoh Road, roughly 18 miles east of US-101 south of Forks), follow the Hoh River Trail upriver — a well-graded, mostly flat rainforest path through old-growth Sitka spruce and big-leaf maple for its first ~12 miles. Checkpoints: Mount Tom Creek camp (~mile 2.5-3, elev ~600-700 ft), the first spot with real views of Mount Tom's icefields across the valley; 5 Mile Island (mile 5); Happy Four shelter camp (mile 5.8); Olympus Guard Station (mile 9, water plus a ranger kiosk, with the High Divide/Bogachiel Peak junction 0.6 mile beyond); Lewis Meadow (mile 10.3); and Elk Lake (mile 14.8, elev ~2,500 ft), the last reliable camp and a common high-camp/turnaround point. Past Elk Lake the trail steepens hard, gaining roughly 1,800 ft in the final 2.3 miles to Glacier Meadows (mile 17.1-17.6, elev ~4,200-4,300 ft), the standard high camp for both Mount Olympus and Mount Tom objectives. Around mile 17.5 a major washout forces a fixed-ladder descent/crossing (roughly 100 ft, rope-assisted, one person on the ladder at a time) — treat this as the approach trail's own crux hazard, not just a nuisance. From Glacier Meadows, take the Lateral Moraine Trail (~0.8 mile, +800 ft) up onto the moraine crest for the first full view of the Blue Glacier and the terrain beyond; a shorter Terminal Moraine Trail (0.4 mile) offers an alternate viewpoint. From the moraine, glacier travel begins: descend onto and cross the (crevassed) Blue Glacier roped as a team, then climb the broad snow dome (Snow Dome) that most Olympus parties also ascend. Rather than continuing toward Olympus's summit, the Mount Tom line splits off Snow Dome and traverses horizontally along the snowy upper massif, staying below the main Olympus peaks, until it connects with Mount Tom's own White Glacier (on its NE slope) or drops via the Hubert Glacier on the south side to reach the Mount Tom-Olympus connecting ridge. Expect open crevasses on both the Blue and White/Hubert Glaciers requiring standard roped glacier travel, and a final class 3-4 scramble over loose rock to the summit block. Water is abundant along the Hoh River Trail itself (river and creek crossings throughout) but is glacier-melt/snowmelt only above Glacier Meadows, so plan treatment accordingly. Round-trip distance on file (~69 km/43 mi) reflects the long valley approach plus the extended high-massif traverse to Tom and back — well beyond a standard Olympus summit day. An alternate approach comes from the Bailey Range side: follow the Sol Duc River/High Divide trail system to Cat Basin, then the off-trail Bailey Range crest (over Stephen Peak, Mount Pulitzer, Mount Fitzhenry, and Ferry Basin) to reach the Mount Tom-Olympus col area, rather than via the Hoh River/Blue Glacier.

wa_navaho_peak_south_slopes — South Slopes  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. South ridge to the summit

--- approach, in full ---
Start at the Stafford Creek Trailhead (Trail #1359), Cle Elum Ranger District, Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest, at the end of North Fork Teanaway River Road (FR 9737) past 29 Pines Campground — a small, often-overflowing lot for roughly 20 cars (Northwest Forest Pass required). From the trailhead, Stafford Creek Trail #1359 climbs at a gentle-to-moderate grade through dry ponderosa/lodgepole forest, staying close to Stafford Creek for the first mile (a short rerouted, steeper switchback section around 0.5 mile bypasses old avalanche-damaged tread). The trail continues gaining gradually along the creek — figure roughly 3,000 ft of gain spread over the approach — reaching a signed junction with the Standup Creek Trail (#1369) at about 3.5–3.8 miles; this crossing of Stafford Creek is the last reliable, easy water source before the high country and is worth topping off here. Bear right (east/north) at the junction to stay on the Navaho Pass approach. Around 4.5 miles the trail opens into a large, marshy subalpine meadow with established campsites and a feeder stream — the usual base camp for parties splitting the trip over two days, and another water source. Beyond camp the trail crosses the barren, eroded 'Moon Landing' stretch (sparse vegetation, serpentine/greenstone talus) before a final push to Navaho Pass (~6,000 ft) around mile 5. From the pass, leave the maintained trail and traverse/climb east along the broad south ridge of Navaho Peak; this is an easy, mostly open Class 2 scramble on rock and heather with no technical exposure, gaining the final 1,100–1,200 ft to the 7,223 ft summit over about 0.7–0.8 mile. A commonly used shortcut leaves the main trail where two creek forks merge around the 3-mile mark, climbing steep, loose slopes directly up the northeast-facing drainage to the col below Navaho's southeast ridge — it saves close to 2 miles of trail hiking but is steeper, unmaintained, and involves loose scree/dirt, so most parties use it only for one direction of the trip. Total car-to-summit is on the order of 11–13 miles round trip with about 4,000–4,200 ft of gain depending on which junction/shortcut combination is used. Note: as of 2026 this trail corridor has had closures tied to Labor Mountain Fire and storm damage — verify current Forest Service trail status before heading in. A second off-trail variant leaves the trail right after the Stafford Creek crossing and climbs directly into the open bowl between Earl Peak and Navaho Peak to about 5,000 ft, scrambles north to the ridgeline near 6,000 ft, then follows the west/southwest ridge to the summit — a different line than the northeast-drainage shortcut.

wa_sheep_gap_mountain_scramble — Standard Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Weden Pass traverse and northeast ridge
  2. Final summit block

--- approach, in full ---
From the Barlow Pass Trailhead (~2,360 ft, on the Mountain Loop Highway about 19.4-19.5 miles east of the Verlot Public Service Center / ~30.5 miles east of Granite Falls; Northwest Forest Pass required), walk south on the flat, gated Monte Cristo Road for roughly 1.5-1.7 miles, paralleling the Sauk River. The Weden Creek / Gothic Basin Trail #724 (an old miner's trail) splits off on the right just before the road's Sauk River crossing. Trail 724 climbs steadily from about 2,300 ft, fords Weden Creek (a real crossing that can be difficult during snowmelt or after rain), then switchbacks through forest and talus to roughly 5,000 ft over about 3.3 miles, passing beneath Foggy Lake/Gothic Basin around the 4.5-mile mark. Leave the maintained trail here and continue west on a flagged boot path past a first pond, then a second, smaller pool, re-crossing the Weden Creek outlet before traversing south — side-hilling steep talus and heather benches between about 4,800 and 5,200 ft — for just over a mile to Weden Pass (5,120 ft). From Weden Pass, traverse southwest toward the notch on the north side of Sheep Gap's main summit, dropping to around 4,900 ft on the traverse, then climb the northeast ridge toward the middle/main summit. Terrain is class 2-3 talus and heather until the final summit block, which turns into sustained class 3-4 rock and brush with one short step requiring a rope belay. Round trip is nearly 19 km (~12 miles) with substantial cumulative gain concentrated in the last few miles, so plan on a very long day (10-17 hours per available trip reports) or camp overnight in Gothic Basin. Key hazards: the Weden Creek ford, loose rock and brush on the class 3/4 sections, and exposed, sometimes-wet talus traverses between Weden Pass and the summit — a helmet is recommended.

wa_snowking_mountain_standard — Standard Route / Snowking Glacier  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Traverse to the east side and onto the glacier
  2. Snowking Glacier
  3. Summit block

--- approach, in full ---
Drive Cascade River Road ~14.2 miles from Marblemount and turn right onto FR-1570 (Kindy Creek Road). The road crosses the Cascade River on a concrete bridge; within roughly 0.4 mile the right fork is blocked by boulders/washout and parking is at that Y-junction (around 1,160-1,200ft). From here the true 'approach' begins as a road-walk: follow the closed roadbed (windfall, brush encroachment, washouts that keep it permanently closed to vehicles) for about 2-3 miles, gaining roughly 1,000ft, to a small clearing/end-of-road firepit near 2,300ft. Watch on the left for a steep, easy-to-miss climbers' path leaving the old roadbed into the trees - this is unsigned and has no official trail number. The path climbs steeply through brush and then old-growth forest on a boot-beaten tread over roots and rock, with no reliable water until well upslope, so top off at the last Cascade River-side crossing if possible. Around 4,800ft there's a boggy bench with a tent spot (first realistic camp/rest option and an intermittent water source). Near 5,200ft the path forks at a signed-by-cairn junction where a fainter 'fisherman's trail' drops toward Found Lake; stay right/uphill on the climbers' path, which steepens over Point 5,791 and follows the Kindy Ridge crest past Found Peak (~5,400ft) and Point 5,695. The ridge undulates - expect several short ups and downs rather than continuous gain - before dropping slightly to the meadows and tarn on the east side of Cyclone Lake (roughly 5,500-6,200ft), the standard high camp with reliable water and the best campsites on the route. From camp, traverse below or over Peak 5,860 heading toward Snowking's east side, then gain the Snowking Glacier proper. In late season this is a straightforward snow/heather slope (~1,900ft over about 1.3 miles to the summit), but by mid-to-late summer the glacier opens up with exposed blue ice and real crevasses - rope up for glacier travel and carry basic crevasse-rescue gear rather than treating it as a snow plod. A final short scree/talus traverse and easy class 2-3 scramble gains the broad summit block at 7,433 ft. Most parties treat this as a two-day trip (car to Cyclone Lake camp, then summit day), though strong parties in good conditions do it car-to-car in 12-14 hours.
