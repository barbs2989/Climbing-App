wa_bedal_peak_standard — Standard Scramble  [scrambling]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Cliff band below the summit massif (crux)
  2. Descending traverse east to the ridge
  3. Snow and granite slabs to the east ridge
  4. Final slab traverse below the summit cliffs

--- approach, in full ---
From the FR 4096 spur (reached via the drive already on file — Mountain Loop Highway to just past Bedal Campground, then up FR 4096), the actual hike starts at an unsigned, unmaintained climber's boot path around 2,100–2,200 ft, roughly 1.3 miles up the spur road near its second or third switchback. The trailhead is marked only by a small cairn/rock pile in the roadside grass and is notoriously easy to drive past — worth GPS-marking on the way in. The path drops into forest and follows Merry Brook (also called Merry Creek) upstream — the last reliable water on route — before swinging north out of the drainage to gain a forested ridge around 2,600 ft. Expect a genuinely unmaintained track with blowdown, brush, and a few faint braids; pink flagging tied roughly every 100 yards helps confirm you're on line, but parties still regularly lose the path, so download a GPS track before you go. Above the ridge the trail climbs through a first boulder field around 3,500 ft (moss-covered talus, easiest when snow-free), passes above a small tarn (Nels Lake) with a cascade audible below, then reaches a second boulder field near 4,200 ft that often holds snow into summer, with views opening north to Spring Mountain around 4,300 ft. By roughly 4,450 ft the route is typically on solid snow in early/mid-season. The technical crux comes around 4,900–5,200 ft at a cliff band below the summit massif: parties look for a ramp/notch line rather than the more direct gully (a moat-like gap has been found there and is best avoided), and the rock is mossy and loose in spots, occasionally icy where snow meets rock — a short hand-line is sometimes used here by less experienced climbers. A short descending traverse east through overgrown brush and devil's club near 5,200 ft regains the ridge above treeline, after which it's a straightforward snow climb and crossing of low-angle granite slabs to the east ridge — an easy, minimally-exposed Class 2–3 scramble toward the summit. The final obstacle, just below the summit cliffs, is a roughly 30-foot traverse of exposed but positive Class 2–3 slabs with a bad runout, slippery when wet and often protected with a hand line by cautious parties.

wa_dome_peak_dome_glacier — Dome Glacier  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Dome Glacier to the 8,560 ft notch

--- approach, in full ---
From the Downey Creek Campground trailhead (~1,450 ft) on Suiattle River Road (FR 26), take the Downey Creek Trail #768, staying on the east bank of Downey Creek through old-growth forest. The trail is rough and lightly maintained — expect brush, downed timber, and washed-out sections; a footlog or ford may be needed at Bachelor Creek (~6.5 mi in), which has been scoured by past avalanches that swept debris across the valley and buried sections of trail. Beyond Bachelor Creek the trail climbs steeply out of the valley toward Cub Lake, gaining roughly 4,000+ ft from the trailhead; this stretch crosses open avalanche paths below Spire Point that should be assessed for stability in early-season snow. Camp is typically made in meadows southeast of Cub Lake (~5,300 ft) or, better positioned for summit day, up on the ridge above at ~6,200 ft (add ~1 hr from the lake). Reported car-to-camp time is 9-11 hours given the distance, brush, and elevation gain — budget this as a genuine multi-day approach, not a day hike-in. From high camp, a boot path crosses the south ridge toward Spire Point, then the route drops ~200 ft and traverses cross-country (roughly 80° bearing) below cliff bands, over glacier-polished slabs (bare rock in late season, snow-covered early season) and a boulder/snow slope, to gain the base of the Dome Glacier. This traverse is unmarked route-finding on exposed, sometimes loose terrain — a map/compass or GPS track is strongly recommended. Rope up for the glacier crossing: follow roughly a 115° bearing up-glacier, working through visible crevasse bands, to a notch at ~8,560 ft just north of the summit block.

wa_east_mcmillan_spire_west_ridge — West Ridge / Southwest Face  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Snow gully to the saddle
  2. Ledge system and rib variation to the saddle
  3. West ridge crest to the summit

--- approach, in full ---
From SR 20 near Newhalem, park at the Goodell Creek Group Campground (~600 ft) just east of Goodell Creek. The climbers' path starts behind a large boulder at the back of the campground and climbs steeply north through forest, gaining a timbered ridge crest around 3,200 ft within the first 1.5-2 miles; two creek crossings low on the path can run high and fast on hot afternoons from snowmelt. The tread then eases onto roughly 4 miles of rougher, brushier but less steep going along the ridge (expect deadfall and a handful of washouts - this is an unmaintained boot path, not a maintained trail) before reaching an obvious cairn around mile 3.5-4 where the route angles right/south to follow above Terror Creek. From there, benches of heather, slabs and rock lead up into Terror Basin (~5,800 ft, roughly 5,200 ft of gain and 6-7 miles in), the standard base camp for McMillan/Inspiration objectives, with reliable running water and flat tent sites. From camp, a long ascending traverse across snow, slabs and heather (crossing a shallow snow-fed depression near 5,500 ft) gains the Terror Glacier below West McMillan Spire - travel is often unroped in firm late-season conditions but crevasses can open, so assess conditions and carry basic glacier gear (axe, crampons). Continue up glacier/snow toward the col between West and East McMillan Spires; the standard line follows a steep snow gully that steepens to roughly 40 degrees for the final 200-300 ft to the saddle. Parties who prefer rock over steep snow can instead follow a reddish, rightward-trending ledge system and a short 4th-class rib on the true-left side of the gully to reach the same saddle. From the saddle, easy but exposed 3rd-class scrambling along the connecting ridge crest (the 'west ridge') leads directly to East McMillan Spire's 7,992 ft summit.

wa_lemah_mountain_east_route — East Route (Main Peak)  [alpine]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Cliff step onto the southeast snowfields
  2. Cliff bands to the Lemah Two / Main Peak col
  3. Summit block step from the col

--- approach, in full ---
Start at the Pete Lake Trailhead (aka Cooper Lake / Cooper River trailhead, ~2,800') at the end of Cooper Lake Road (FS-46); a Northwest Forest Pass is required to park. Follow the Cooper River Trail (Trail #1323) roughly 4 flat, forested miles to Pete Lake (~2,980'). Continue along the lakeshore and past its camps for about another 1.2 miles, staying left at the trail forks, to reach the Pacific Crest Trail junction. Turn north (right) on the PCT for approximately 1 mile as it climbs into the Lemah Creek drainage. Leave the maintained trail near the Lemah Creek crossing (the PCT footbridge here has been reported washed out in recent years) and head west/cross-country toward Upper Lemah Meadows — an intermittent climbers' path and sparse cairns mark the way for the first ~1,000 feet before fading into open terrain. From there it's another 1-2 miles of off-trail travel up the Lemah Creek valley — past small waterfalls and swimming holes, through several creek crossings, and some brushy slide-alder sections — gaining to a meadow camp around 3,600'. Most parties camp here or continue up the valley along the creek to about 5,000' where the glacier remnant/snowfinger terminates. From there climb to roughly 6,200' and exit left (south) up a short cliff step onto the glacial remnants/snowfields on the peak's southeast side. Traverse to just southwest of the main summit and climb a series of Class 2-3 cliff bands to the col between Lemah Two and the Main (East) Peak — this is the technical crux of the approach/climb and the point most trip reports flag for route-finding and loose rock. Total is roughly 16 miles round trip with ~4,700' of gain, and parties commonly take 8-10+ hours from trailhead to summit, so many split it into a two-day trip with a camp near 3,600'. Hazards: cold, sometimes thigh-deep early-season creek crossings on Lemah Creek; brush and downed timber off-trail; loose, exposed Class 2-3 scrambling in the cliff bands; and a genuine route-finding crux locating the correct gully/cliff-band line up to the col, especially in fog, low visibility, or lingering snow. From the Lemah Two/Main col, a short, exposed step of easy 5th-class climbing guards the final summit block.

wa_mount_custer_standard — Standard Route / South Side  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Broken buttress southwest of Lake Ouzel
  2. Redoubt Glacier to the 8,260-ft notch
  3. Summit gully (crux)
  4. South Ridge to the summit

--- approach, in full ---
From the Depot Creek trailhead (reached via the unsigned dirt spur off Chilliwack Lake Road in BC — see road note), follow the old trail south into the US: about 200 yards through old growth to the border obelisk and trail register, then continue on the fading Depot Creek path up the valley. The first stretch follows an old roadbed for under a mile before it vanishes into forest; for roughly 3 miles the route weaves through timber and brush with no consistent tread. At the valley's headwall (~4,800 ft) the trail parallels a waterfall on Depot Creek — expect a stream crossing at its base, wet slabs, and at least two fixed/dangling handlines on the exposed slab sections beside the falls (a real fall hazard if wet or icy). Above the falls the path becomes fainter through talus and brush in the open basin below Mount Redoubt before reaching Lake Ouzel (5,700 ft, ~8-9 miles and ~3,500 ft gain from the car, 5-6 hours), the standard base camp on flat sandy benches. From camp, scramble up the large broken buttress southwest of the lake (cairned to aid the return); partway up the buttress traverse far climber's-right, then continue up to gain the Redoubt Glacier easily. Cross/skirt the glacier (crevasse hazard, rope up if conditions warrant) to reach the obvious SW basin near 7,700 ft below Custer's summit, then continue to the 8,260-ft notch immediately south of the true summit. A short, vertical, 6-8-ft-wide rock gully (with occasional ice) breaks through here — this is the technical crux of the ascent. Above it, follow the loose, chossy South Ridge (widely regarded as some of the worst rock quality in the Cascades — test every hold) the remaining short distance to the summit.

wa_mount_fury_east_southeast_glaciers — Southeast Glaciers (Standard)  [mountaineering]

--- climbing_route sections ---
  1. Northeast Ridge crest from Luna Pass
  2. Heather chute and traverse to the rocky rib
  3. Southeast Glacier
  4. Summit snowfield and arête

--- approach, in full ---
From the Ross Dam Trailhead at Happy Flats on SR 20 (~milepost 134, 2,150 ft), it's about 1 mile down to the Ross Lake water taxi dock; cross the lake by taxi (or paddle) to Big Beaver Landing. From the landing, follow the Big Beaver Creek Trail 11.5 miles up-valley — a dry, well-maintained, largely flat trail taking roughly 5 hours, passing Luna Camp around the 4.7-hour mark. About 29 minutes beyond Luna Camp, a large (~15-inch-diameter) log crossing of Big Beaver Creek marks the turnoff: leave the trail here and head up the Access Creek drainage. This next mile is a real bushwhack, gaining roughly 1,900 ft (2,400 ft to 4,300 ft): start in valley-bottom devil's club, cross to the creek's north side around 3,800 ft where brush pinches in on both banks, then climb through moderately dense huckleberry brush before the final half-mile of talus into Access Basin — watch for deadfall, thick undercover, and biting insects. From Access Basin, a long, steep, loose scree chute gains ~1,800 ft to 6,100-ft Access Col (2-3 hours; can be brutally hot with no shade in the chute). From the col, traverse 3-4 miles of notoriously steep heather (an axe is worthwhile for security on the short, steep sections) and talus to Luna Pass, where most parties camp near lingering snow patches for water. From Luna Pass, skirt a rock knob that towers ~100 ft over the pass via a broad ledge on its northwest face to reach the surprisingly gentle Northeast Ridge crest, then follow the ridge's many short ups and downs — including a ~25-ft rock step with a climbable vertical crack (Class 3) — toward Fury. Where the ridge crest drops away, descend a steep, narrow heather chute (~500 ft) into a talus basin around 6,500 ft, then contour southwest across talus, heather, and polished slabs to a rocky rib that blocks easy passage; the lower of two ledge options (exposed scrambling on wet, mossy rock and steep heather) is generally preferred to the looser, more exposed upper ledge. Beyond the rib, rope up for the crevassed Southeast Glacier (starting ~6,500-7,000 ft): ascend its true right/climber's-right side on moderate (20-40°) snow, working around a bergschrund near 7,900 ft (usually passable on its left side). The final summit snowfield steepens from ~30-40° to nearly 50° near the top — firm enough for front-pointing but not always secure for flat-footing — with snow flukes commonly used for running belays before a short snow arête leads to the summit rocks.
