# Glacier travel on a normal summit day — review rule (2026-11-23)

Owner, 2026-11-23: routes with no glacier must not be warned about an overnight freeze, weak snow bridges or the
freezing level "when there's usually not those when people typically climb that route". The Conditions card
decides this from two facts per route. Each reviewed route gets both.

A NORMAL DAY is a day in the season parties usually climb THIS route by THIS line (for most Washington summer
routes July to September; for a spring snow climb or a ski-season volcano route, its own usual season).

## `glacier` — does the party travel ON a glacier with crevasse hazard?

- **yes** — the normal line crosses or climbs a glacier where crevasses, snow bridges, a bergschrund or an
  icefall are a real hazard and parties normally rope up for it (Easton, Coleman, Sulphide, Emmons, Quien Sabe,
  Inspiration, Boston, Sahale Glacier above the arm, Eldorado, Blue Glacier, etc.).
- **no** — there is no glacier on the line: the glacier is only seen, named in the peak or trailhead ("Glacier
  Peak" area names, "Glacier Basin"), on a different route, or is a stagnant remnant / permanent snowfield with
  no crevasse hazard that parties cross unroped. "Picket" as gear for a steep snow slope is not glacier evidence.
- **unsure** — cannot tell from the text plus what you can verify. Unsure KEEPS the glacier warnings.

## `snowInSeason` — does the party cross or climb snow at all on a normal day?

- **yes** — permanent snow, a snow couloir, snow col or snow descent that holds through the normal season; ice
  axe / crampons required for the normal ascent; any glacier (`glacier: yes` implies `snowInSeason: yes`); a
  route whose normal season IS the snow season (spring couloirs, winter routes, volcano ski lines).
- **no** — every snow mention is early-season, "lingers into June/July", after a storm, optional gear, a place
  name, a road closure, or a neighbouring route.
- **unsure** — the text cannot tell. Unsure KEEPS the warnings.

## Asymmetry

Wrongly dropping a warning from a route that does cross a glacier or snow is the dangerous error. Answer "no"
only on positive evidence. Evidence is the route's own sentences first; a well-documented fact about the peak
and line (a guidebook-level fact, or one confirmed by a web search) may settle what the text leaves open, and
the `why` must say which it was.
