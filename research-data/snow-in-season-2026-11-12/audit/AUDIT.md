# Online audit of the "dry in season" calls (2026-11-13)

PR #3268 removed the freezing-level / refreeze / snow-softening flags from 361 alpine-rock and scramble
routes judged, from their OWN catalog text, to cross no snow or glacier on a normal peak-season summit day
(rule: ../RULE.md — peak season; snow that only lingers into early or midsummer does not count).

This audit checks those calls against outside sources. A wrong "dry" hides a real safety flag, so the
question per route is: **in a normal year, at the route's usual peak season (typically late July–September
in the Cascades/Sierra/Rockies), does the standard way up, the climb itself, or the standard descent cross
snow, firn, névé or glacier?**

Verdicts per route:
- `confirmed-dry` — a source describes the peak-season approach/climb/descent as snow-free, or describes
  snow only as early-season / avoidable.
- `snow-in-season` — a source shows the party normally crosses snow or glacier in peak season (a snowfield
  on the descent in August, axe/crampons recommended for the normal summer descent, a permanent snowfield).
  These go back to keeping the flags.
- `unclear` — sources disagree, or say "some years".
- `not-found` — no usable source in the budget.

Sources: trip reports with dates, guidebook-derived pages, NPS/USFS, WTA hike pages, Steph Abegg,
climberkyle, Mountain Project (if fetch works), 14ers.com, SummitPost (often 403 — skip, do not work
around). No browser tools, no bypassing blocks.
